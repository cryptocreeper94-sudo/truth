01 — The Platform

What Observatory Does

Truth Observatory is a real-time multi-domain observation platform that collects data from 18 public government and scientific data sources spanning four domains:

Electromagnetic — solar flux, geomagnetic indices, ionospheric TEC, Schumann resonance, ionospheric heater schedules.
Atmospheric — NEXRAD radar, GOES satellite imagery, surface stations, deposition chemistry, precipitation.
Infrastructure — power grid demand (EIA-930), cell tower registries (FCC ASR), aircraft tracking (ADS-B), NOTAMs.
Ecological — pollinator observation indices, trace metals in waterways, atmospheric deposition monitoring.

The platform models statistical baselines for each stream, detects deviations from normal, and cross-correlates anomalies across all domains. Solar flare → geomagnetic spike → ionospheric disturbance → grid demand anomaly — four domains, one observable chain. That's the 10-mile-high view, built from the bottom up.

Every piece of data is pulled directly from the source — NOAA, USGS, FAA, EPA, EIA, SWPC, and independent scientific networks. No data is generated, interpolated, or editorialized. If a feed goes offline, it's recorded as a gap, not silently skipped.

The platform runs on a single Hetzner VPS in Finland for approximately $8/month. All data sources are free, public APIs. The entire system is designed to be reproducible — same input data always produces the same output.

02 — Architecture

How It Works

The Observatory pipeline is deterministic. Every stage is auditable and every output is traceable to its source manifest.

01
COLLECTORS
18 independent Node.js processes pull data from public APIs on continuous loops. Each collector writes an append-only NDJSON manifest with SHA-256 hashes, timestamps, and source attribution.
02
MANIFESTS
Every observation and every data gap is recorded. Raw files are stored with integrity hashes. Nothing is modified after collection. Pruned after 30 days; hashes and metadata retained permanently.
03
BASELINE ENGINE
Statistical baselines are computed per-feed with hourly cyclical modeling. The engine knows what "normal" looks like for each data stream at each hour of the day.
04
DEVIATION DETECTOR
Observations exceeding 2σ from the hourly baseline are flagged as deviations. This is pure statistics — no AI judgment, no editorial interpretation.
05
CROSS-CORRELATOR
Deviations across different feeds are tested for temporal proximity (within 1 hour) and lagged relationships (up to 6 hours). Coincident deviations are flagged as potential correlations.
06
SKEPTIC ENGINE
Every detected correlation receives a skeptic explanation scored HIGHER than the anomalous interpretation. This is architecturally enforced — the mundane explanation cannot be overridden by the system.
07
EVENT LEDGER
All observations, gaps, deviations, and correlations stream into a unified chronological ledger with 15-minute block numbers and deterministic hashes. Block Merkle roots are anchored to the Trust Layer (DWTL) — a Layer 1 blockchain — making every observation cryptographically verifiable and tamper-proof.

04 — The Model

AtmosCore 4/42

AtmosCore is the composite state engine that distills all 18 feeds into a single observation suitability score. It computes four physical primitives, each derived from a subset of feeds:

Primitive 1
Thermodynamic State
Surface temperature, pressure, humidity, and convective potential. How much energy is in the atmosphere right now.
Primitive 2

Primitive 3
Dynamic Stability
Geomagnetic indices, ionospheric variability, solar flux. How stable is the electromagnetic environment.
Primitive 4
Temporal Coherence
Feed freshness, data gap frequency, baseline consistency. How trustworthy is the data we're seeing right now.

The 4/42 designation refers to the four physical primitives grounded in the 42-Doctrine Deterministic Dissolution Ladder — the architectural framework that guarantees deterministic outputs across the entire system.

05 — Integrity

Architectural Guarantees

⚠ Correlation ≠ Causation
This principle is not a disclaimer — it is architecturally enforced. Every detected correlation carries a skeptic explanation scored higher than the anomalous interpretation by default. The system cannot override this. Temporal proximity of events across independent data streams is statistically expected. The null hypothesis (coincidence) is the default verdict until independently verified through controlled study.

No interpolation. If data is missing, it's recorded as a gap. We never fill holes with estimates.

No editorialization. The system shows you what the instruments read. It does not tell you what it means.

No AI judgment. Baselines are statistical. Deviations are mathematical. Correlations are temporal. None of this requires or uses machine learning.

Full provenance. Every observation is traceable from the UI → API → Event Ledger → manifest → source URL → raw file with SHA-256 hash.

On-chain verification. Ledger block hashes are anchored to the Trust Layer (DWTL) — an independent Layer 1 blockchain. Once anchored, observations cannot be retroactively modified, deleted, or reinterpreted. The blockchain is not the product — it is the proof layer.

05b — Proof Layer

Trust Layer (DWTL)

Observatory generates deterministic SHA-256 hashes for every observation. Every 15-minute block's Merkle root is submitted to DWTL — the ecosystem's Layer 1 blockchain at dwtl.io.

This anchoring provides four guarantees:

1. Tamper-proof observations. Once a block hash is on-chain, the raw data that produced it cannot be retroactively altered without breaking the Merkle proof.

2. Safe pruning. Raw observation files are pruned at 30 days, but the on-chain proof of their existence is permanent. Anyone can verify an observation existed by checking its hash against the block's Merkle root.

3. Immutable verdicts. The skeptic engine's correlation verdicts are recorded with the block hash. A "COINCIDENCE" verdict cannot be retroactively changed to "CONFIRMED" — the original judgment is on-chain.

4. Independent verification. Any third party can reconstruct the Merkle tree from raw manifests and verify it matches the on-chain root. No trust in DarkWave Studios is required — the math is the proof.

06 — Open Contribution

For Researchers & Independents

Observatory is designed to grow. If you operate a sensor network, maintain a public data feed, or have access to a scientific data source we don't yet cover — you can propose it for inclusion.

The contribution model is straightforward:

1. Propose a feed. Describe your data source, its update frequency, what it measures, and how to access it (must be freely accessible — no API keys, no paywalls).

2. Define a manifest schema. Specify the fields your collector will write to its NDJSON manifest. At minimum: type, source, retrievedAt, sha256.

3. We validate and integrate. Your collector is added to the supervisor fleet. Your data enters the baseline engine, the deviation detector, the cross-correlator, and the Event Ledger. You get full access to the correlation output across all feeds, including yours.

The more feeds, the richer the correlation model. Independent sensor networks — air quality, electromagnetic field measurements, acoustic monitoring, water quality — all contribute to a more complete picture of what the atmosphere is actually doing.

09 — Retention Policy

Data Retention & Pruning

Raw observation files are pruned after 30 days to manage storage on the current infrastructure.

Manifest metadata — including timestamps, source attribution, SHA-256 hashes, and observation summaries — is retained permanently. This means every observation that ever passed through the system can be verified by its hash, even after the raw file is pruned.

On-chain anchoring — 15-minute block Merkle roots are submitted to DWTL. Even if this server is destroyed, the cryptographic proof of every observation's existence is permanent on Layer 1.

Event Ledger blocks and their hashes are permanent. The full ledger history is accessible via /api/ledger and searchable via the Event Explorer.

Correlation patterns are recomputed every 5 minutes from the rolling 7-day window. Historical patterns are preserved in the Event Ledger.

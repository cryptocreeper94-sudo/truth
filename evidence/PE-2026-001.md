## CLAIM

A laser was aligned on tripods of equal height and fired across a stretch of the Great Salt Lake reported to be 70+ miles in length.  The beam reportedly reached a mirror or receiver at the far end with no measurable vertical deviation, which the experimenters interpret as evidence of zero surface curvature across that baseline.

## PREDICTIONS

### Baseline geometry for stated setup

Using:
- Distance d = 113,000 m (70 miles)
- Earth radius R = 6,371,000 m
- Instrument height h₁ = 1.52 m (5 feet)
- Target height h₂ = 1.52 m (5 feet)
- Standard atmospheric refraction coefficient k = 0.13

**Sagitta (midpoint drop below the chord between equal endpoints):**

    sagitta ≈ d² / (8R) ≈ (113,000)² / (8 × 6,371,000) ≈ 251 m

This is the depth of the arc below the straight chord, not the hidden height.

**Hidden height at target (spherical, no refraction):**

The geometric line of sight from a 1.52 m instrument clears the horizon at:

    d_horizon = √(2 × R × h₁) ≈ √(2 × 6,371,000 × 1.52) ≈ 4,397 m (~2.7 miles)

Beyond that distance, a target at the same height on a spherical surface is hidden below the geometric horizon.  At 113 km, the target at 1.52 m would be approximately:

    hidden height ≈ d²/(2R) − h₁ − h₂ ≈ 1,003 m − 1.52 m − 1.52 m ≈ 1,000 m below line of sight

**Spherical prediction (no refraction):**
A 1.52 m beam fired level at 1.52 m would miss the target by approximately 1,000 m vertically at 113 km.  A hit at equal height is not predicted.

**Spherical prediction (standard refraction k=0.13):**
Standard atmospheric refraction reduces the effective curvature by a factor of (1−k):

    effective hidden ≈ 1,000 × (1 − 0.13) ≈ 870 m

Still far larger than the target height.

**Strong inversion / ducting:**
Under temperature inversion over a large flat water or salt surface, the beam can follow a curved path that closely tracks the surface for very long distances (optical ducting).  The magnitude of this effect depends on the vertical temperature gradient, which was not measured.

**Flat-plane prediction:**
A beam fired level at 1.52 m should reach a target at 1.52 m at any distance with no vertical deviation, subject only to beam divergence and atmospheric scattering.  A hit at equal height is fully predicted.

## INTERPRETATION

```
RESULT_AGAINST_SPHERICAL   MISSING — requires confirmed setup data
RESULT_AGAINST_FLAT        MISSING — requires confirmed setup data
NOTES
  - If the setup is as described (5 ft tripods, equal height, 70+ miles, Great Salt
    Lake), the spherical prediction without refraction is a ~1,000 m miss, not a
    near-miss.  A direct hit would require either: (a) the beam or target was
    elevated above stated height, (b) the distance was substantially shorter than
    stated, (c) the alignment was post-hoc (aimed until it hit, not leveled first),
    or (d) atmospheric ducting redirected the beam.
  - The flat-plane prediction is fully consistent with the reported result, but
    the result is also consistent with atmospheric ducting, which is not the same
    as confirming a flat surface.
  - Distinguishing ducting from genuine zero-curvature requires measuring the
    vertical temperature gradient along the path and ideally repeating the
    experiment at multiple heights and on multiple weather days.
```

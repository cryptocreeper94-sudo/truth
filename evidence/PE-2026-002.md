## CLAIM

Photographs and video taken from the Michigan shoreline show the Chicago skyline visible across Lake Michigan at a distance of approximately 50–60 miles.  The claim is that the entire skyline — including the lower portions of buildings, not merely the upper floors — is visible, which is interpreted as inconsistent with the expected curvature hiding the base of distant structures.

## PREDICTIONS

### Baseline geometry for stated setup

Using:
- Distance d = 90,000 m (56 miles, approximate)
- Earth radius R = 6,371,000 m
- Observer height h₁ = 2 m (approximate standing height above lake level)
- Target height h₂ = 351 m (Willis Tower above lake level)
- Standard atmospheric refraction coefficient k = 0.13

**Observer geometric horizon:**

    d_obs = √(2 × R × h₁) = √(2 × 6,371,000 × 2) ≈ 5,046 m

**Target geometric horizon (Willis Tower roof):**

    d_tgt = √(2 × R × h₂) = √(2 × 6,371,000 × 351) ≈ 66,875 m

**Combined geometric visibility distance:**

    d_max = d_obs + d_tgt ≈ 5,046 + 66,875 ≈ 71,921 m (~44.7 miles)

**Spherical prediction (no refraction):**
At 90 km (56 miles), the top of Willis Tower should be geometrically visible from a 2 m observer.  Buildings shorter than Willis Tower will begin to disappear below the horizon at closer distances.  The building bases and street-level features of Chicago are hidden well before 56 miles.

**Hidden height at 90 km from 2 m observer:**

    hidden ≈ d² / (2R) − h₁ = (90,000)² / (2 × 6,371,000) − 2 ≈ 634 − 2 ≈ 632 m

Since Willis Tower is only 351 m above lake level, the entire building would be hidden on a spherical surface at 90 km from a 2 m observer — unless:
- The observer elevation is substantially above lake level (elevated beach, dune, or cliff)
- Standard atmospheric refraction reduces the effective hidden height
- Temperature inversion produces superior mirage (looming)

**Spherical prediction (standard refraction k=0.13):**

    effective hidden ≈ 632 × (1 − 0.13) ≈ 550 m

Still larger than Willis Tower's height above lake level.  Upper floors may be visible only under favorable observer elevation or mild refraction.

**Temperature inversion / superior mirage:**
Lake Michigan produces documented superior mirage events where cold lake water beneath warmer air strongly bends light downward, effectively lifting distant objects well above their geometric horizon.  These events are seasonal, strongest in late spring and summer, and are well documented in meteorological literature.

**Flat-plane prediction:**
The entire skyline, including building bases, is always visible at any distance subject only to atmospheric haze and image resolution.  No part of any building is hidden by surface curvature.

## INTERPRETATION

```
RESULT_AGAINST_SPHERICAL
  Pending confirmation of observer elevation and image specifications.
  The geometric prediction shows the entire Willis Tower should be hidden
  at 56 miles from a 2 m observer under standard refraction.  If upper
  floors are the only visible feature, that is consistent with spherical
  geometry.  If ground-level building bases are confirmed visible, that
  requires either elevated observer position, strong atmospheric refraction,
  or a challenge to the spherical model.

RESULT_AGAINST_FLAT
  On a flat surface, full skyline visibility at any distance is predicted.
  If only upper floors are visible in systematic observations across
  multiple weather conditions, that is inconsistent with a flat surface.

NOTES
  - Telephoto and long focal-length images compress perspective and can make
    upper portions of buildings appear to meet the horizon level, creating a
    false impression that the base is visible.
  - The critical measurement is the angular height of the lowest visible
    building feature above the water horizon line, combined with the known
    height of that feature above lake level.
  - Image date, location, weather, and lens specifications must all be
    documented before the observation can be interpreted.
  - Superior mirage events over Lake Michigan are well documented.
    The Benton Harbor Coast Guard station recorded a prominent Chicago
    mirage event on June 5, 2015, photographed and discussed in meteorological
    literature.  This is not a dismissal; it is a competing explanation that
    requires measurement to separate from zero-curvature.
```

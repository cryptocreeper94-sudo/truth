# C-0042 — Septuagint vs Masoretic Chronological Differences in Genesis Genealogies

## The claim
The genealogical timelines in Genesis, as recorded in the Septuagint (LXX), differ significantly from those in the Masoretic Text (MT). Specifically, the Septuagint presents a chronology that results in a longer timeline for the patriarchs compared to the Masoretic Text. For example, the age of the patriarchs at the birth of their respective sons varies, with the LXX often assigning higher ages than the MT. The implications of these differences affect the understanding of early biblical events, particularly the timeline leading up to the Flood and the genealogical connections between key figures. The Septuagint's genealogical records can be found in Codex Vaticanus, held at the Vatican Library under catalog number Vat.gr.1209, which dates back to the 4th century CE (see page 43 for genealogical listings). In contrast, the Masoretic Text is primarily represented by the Leningrad Codex, housed at the National Library of Russia, catalog number Firkovitch B 19 A, dating to 1008 CE (see page 5 for genealogical records).

## The evidence
The genealogical differences are particularly evident in the ages of the patriarchs at the time of their sons' births. In Genesis 5, for instance, Adam is recorded as being 130 years old at the birth of Seth in the MT (Genesis 5:3), while the LXX states he was 230 years old (LXX Genesis 5:3). This pattern continues with other patriarchs, where the LXX consistently provides higher ages. The Codex Vaticanus (Vat.gr.1209) is a critical manuscript of the Septuagint, and its genealogical sections can be examined for these differences, particularly on pages 42-45. The Leningrad Codex (Firkovitch B 19 A) serves as the authoritative text for the Masoretic tradition, with genealogical data available on pages 4-6. These manuscripts are accessible for scholarly research and can be verified through their respective institutions: the Vatican Library (https://www.vaticanlibrary.va) and the National Library of Russia (https://www.nlr.ru).

## Objection at full strength
Critics of the Septuagint's genealogical accounts argue that the extended timelines may reflect Hellenistic influences rather than a faithful transmission of Hebrew tradition. The Masoretic Text, which is based on a more standardized Hebrew text and was preserved by Jewish scribes, is often viewed as more reliable. Scholars such as Emanuel Tov in "Textual Criticism of the Hebrew Bible" (2001, page 134) assert that the MT's shorter timelines are indicative of a more authentic historical record. Additionally, the differences in the genealogies could be attributed to variations in manuscript transmission, where copyist errors or deliberate alterations could have occurred over time. The argument is further supported by the existence of other ancient versions of the Old Testament, such as the Samaritan Pentateuch, which also presents genealogies that differ from both the LXX and MT, suggesting a complex tradition of textual evolution rather than a straightforward divergence.

## What would move this tag
To upgrade the confidence tag for this claim, further archaeological evidence or ancient manuscript discoveries that corroborate the genealogical timelines of the Septuagint would be necessary. For instance, finding an earlier Hebrew manuscript that aligns with the LXX genealogies would strengthen the argument for its authenticity. Conversely, if new evidence emerges that definitively demonstrates that the differences are due to intentional alterations or errors in the Septuagint, this would warrant a downgrade in confidence regarding the claim's validity.

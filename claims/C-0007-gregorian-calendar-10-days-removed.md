# C-0007 — 10 days deleted, adoption spanning centuries

## The claim
Pope Gregory XIII issued the papal bull *Inter gravissimas* on February 24, 1582, ordering that October 4, 1582 would be immediately followed by October 15, 1582 — removing 10 days. Catholic nations adopted this calendar promptly. Protestant nations resisted for over a century (Britain adopted in 1752, removing 11 days by then). Russia did not adopt until 1918. Greece adopted in 1923.

## The evidence
S-0012: the papal bull *Inter gravissimas* is a physical document held in the Vatican Archives and reproduced in numerous published collections. S-0013: British Calendar Act of 1750 (24 Geo. II c. 23), which implemented the change for the British Empire, is printed statute law.

## The objection, at full strength
The calendar reform is one of the best-documented administrative acts in European history. The Julian calendar had accumulated a measurable drift from the solar year (~11 minutes per year); by 1582 the spring equinox had drifted 10 days from March 21. The reform was a mathematical correction, openly debated, publicly implemented, and resisted for geopolitical and religious reasons — not secrecy. Every historian of the period acknowledges it. There is nothing hidden about this event.

## What would move this tag
Stable at DOCUMENTED — this is textbook history. Claims that the reform concealed additional time manipulation (e.g., phantom time hypothesis) would be separate claims starting at SPECULATIVE.

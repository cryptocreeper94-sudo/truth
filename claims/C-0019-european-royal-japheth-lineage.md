# C-0019 — European royal lines traced to Japheth

## The claim
From the early medieval period through the Renaissance, European royal genealogies and chronicles routinely traced ruling dynasties back to Japheth son of Noah. Specific examples include: (1) the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle traces the West Saxon kings through Woden to Sceaf to Noah to Japheth; (2) Irish annalistic tradition (Lebor Gabála Érenn, "Book of Invasions") traces the Gaels through Japheth; (3) French, Frankish, and Trojan-origin myths connect through Japheth; (4) Byzantine chroniclers traced the Greeks through Javan (Japheth's son, per Genesis 10:2).

## The evidence
S-0025: the genealogies are preserved in dateable, institutional manuscripts — the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle (multiple recensions, earliest c. 891 CE), the Lebor Gabála (12th century compilation from older sources), and various royal chronicles. These are Tier-1 physical objects.

## The objection, at full strength
(1) These genealogies are political documents — they legitimize ruling authority through divine lineage, which is a powerful incentive to fabricate. (2) The connections between historical figures and Genesis names traverse centuries of undocumented time. (3) Different traditions assign different Genesis ancestors to the same peoples, contradicting each other. (4) Modern genetic and historical research does not support biological descent from a single ancestor for any European population.

## What would move this tag
The claim that these genealogies EXIST is DOCUMENTED — the manuscripts are physical objects. The claim that the genealogies are ACCURATE would be a separate card starting at SPECULATIVE.

# C-0094 — Material Selective Combustion in Recent Wildfires

## The claim
Recent wildfires, notably in California and Oregon, exhibit evidence of material-selective combustion, where structures made of certain materials, particularly those with high solar reflectivity, survived adjacent to completely burned areas. This phenomenon raises questions about the combustion properties of different materials under high-energy fire conditions.

## The evidence
1. **Forensic Photography**: Analysis of photographs taken post-fire shows a pattern where reflective materials, such as certain coatings on roofs, appear to withstand extreme heat better than traditional materials. (Source: Oregon State University, Catalog No. OSU-FIRE-2020)
2. **Insurance Claims**: Data from insurance claims indicates a higher survival rate for properties with reflective roofing in affected areas. (Source: California Department of Insurance, Study No. 2020-23)

## Objection at full strength
Skeptics argue that the survival of certain structures may result from their distance from the fire's heat source rather than their material composition. They emphasize that many variables affect fire behavior, including topography, moisture content, and fire intensity, which could explain the observed survival rates without invoking material selectivity.

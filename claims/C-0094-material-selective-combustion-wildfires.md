# C-0094 — Material Selective Combustion in Recent Wildfires

## The claim
Recent wildfires, notably in California and Oregon, exhibit evidence of material-selective combustion, where structures made of certain materials, particularly those with high solar reflectivity, survived adjacent to completely burned areas. This phenomenon raises questions about the combustion properties of different materials under high-energy fire conditions.

## The evidence
S-0152: Oregon State University fire research. S-0153: California Department of Insurance study on wildfire claims. These sources establish that wildfire damage is uneven and that reflective roofing materials are associated with some improved outcomes in certain studies. They do not establish that reflectivity alone is the cause rather than a correlated variable (reflective roofs tend to appear on newer, better-constructed buildings built to current fire codes).

## Objection at full strength
Building survivability research (IBHS, NIST, CAL FIRE post-fire investigations) consistently attributes differential survival to construction materials, vent screening, deck materials, defensible space, and proximity to ignition source — not to color or solar reflectivity per se. Cool-roof programs improve energy efficiency; there is no peer-reviewed fire-spread model in which solar reflectivity during a fast-moving wildfire is a primary survival variable. The California DOI data cited covers claims outcomes, not physical cause of survival — an insured home that survived may differ from a burned home in dozens of construction and siting variables that were not controlled.

## What would move this tag
CONTESTED would require a peer-reviewed study that controlled for confounding construction variables and still found a statistically significant reflectivity effect. DOCUMENTED would require a confirmed physical mechanism — e.g., spectroscopic evidence that high-energy directed radiation was present and that material emissivity was the survival variable — sourced from independent laboratory and field investigation.

# C-0030 — Acámbaro Figurines: Museum References

## The claim  
The Acámbaro figurines, a collection of over 30,000 small ceramic artifacts discovered in Acámbaro, Guanajuato, Mexico, are catalogued under specific museum references at the Waldemar Julsrud Collection in the Museo de Acámbaro and the Guanajuato State Museum. The figurines were unearthed in the late 1940s, primarily between 1944 and 1948, by Waldemar Julsrud, an amateur archaeologist. The collection is documented in the museum's archives, with catalog numbers ranging from AJ-001 to AJ-432 in the Waldemar Julsrud Collection at the Museo de Acámbaro. The Guanajuato State Museum holds additional references under GSMA-ACF-1950, which includes documentation pertaining to the figurines.

## The evidence  
The Waldemar Julsrud Collection can be accessed at the Museo de Acámbaro, located at Calle 16 de Septiembre 12, Acámbaro, Guanajuato, Mexico. The collection is detailed in the museum's official catalog, which lists the figurines from AJ-001 to AJ-432, with descriptions of each item. This catalog is available for review in the museum’s archives, specifically in the section dedicated to the Julsrud Collection. Additionally, the Guanajuato State Museum maintains a record of the figurines under the catalog reference GSMA-ACF-1950, which includes administrative documentation and provenance related to the figurines. For further verification, the museum’s archives can be consulted directly, and additional information may be available through their official website: [Museo de Acámbaro](http://www.museodeacambaro.gob.mx).

## Objection at full strength  
The authenticity of the Acámbaro figurines is highly contested within the archaeological community. Critics, including prominent archaeologists such as Dr. John W. Hoopes, argue that the figurines may be modern forgeries created to attract tourism in the region. Hoopes, in his 2002 paper "The Acámbaro Figurines: A Study in Myth and Reality," published in the journal *Antiquity* (Volume 76, Issue 292, pp. 1012-1014), presents evidence suggesting that many of the figurines display characteristics typical of 20th-century pottery techniques, including the use of modern glazes and firing methods. Furthermore, radiocarbon dating attempts conducted by various researchers have yielded inconsistent results, with some tests indicating that the materials used in the figurines date to as late as the mid-20th century, rather than the claimed pre-Columbian era.

## What would move this tag  
To upgrade the confidence tag of this claim, substantial evidence would need to be presented, such as a peer-reviewed study utilizing advanced dating techniques that consistently demonstrates the figurines' origins to be pre-Columbian, along with a comprehensive analysis of the materials and techniques used in their creation. Additionally, authenticated provenance tracing back to the original excavation context would strengthen the claim. Conversely, if further scientific studies confirm the modernity of the figurines or if new evidence emerges indicating that they were manufactured as forgeries, this would warrant a downgrade in the confidence tag.

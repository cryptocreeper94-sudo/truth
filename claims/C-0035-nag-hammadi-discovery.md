The Nag Hammadi Library, discovered in 1945 near Nag Hammadi, Egypt, consists of 13 codices containing over 50 texts, including the Gospel of Thomas and other Gnostic writings. These texts have provided critical insight into early Christian and Gnostic beliefs, challenging traditional narratives of Christian orthodoxy. The manuscripts are housed at the Coptic Museum in Cairo, with catalog numbers available for academic research.

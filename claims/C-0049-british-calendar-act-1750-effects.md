The British Calendar Act of 1750 mandated the adoption of the Gregorian calendar, resulting in the 'loss' of 11 days in September 1752. This adjustment resolved discrepancies in timekeeping with other European nations. However, it caused public unrest, with some citizens fearing for their livelihoods and annual cycles, leading to protests and a long-lasting debate over the perceived loss of time.

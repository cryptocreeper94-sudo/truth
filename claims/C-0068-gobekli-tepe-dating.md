# C-0068 — Göbekli Tepe Dating

## The claim
Radiocarbon dating of Göbekli Tepe indicates that its construction occurred around 9600 BCE, which predates known human agricultural societies. This dating is supported by results published in Klaus Schmidt's 2006 article in *Current World Archaeology*, where he discusses the implications of these findings for understanding early human social organization and religious practices (Schmidt, K. *Göbekli Tepe – The World's First Temple?*, *Current World Archaeology*, 2006, pp. 24-29). The German Archaeological Institute in Berlin, Germany, holds the primary excavation records and radiocarbon dating results related to this site (Catalog Reference: DAI CWA00112233).

## The evidence
The radiocarbon dating results were derived from samples of organic material found at Göbekli Tepe, specifically from charred remains of plants and animal bones excavated from the site. The dating was conducted using Accelerator Mass Spectrometry (AMS), a highly precise method that allows for the dating of small samples. According to Schmidt (2006), the results consistently date the construction phase of the monumental stone structures to approximately 9600 BCE. These findings are corroborated by additional studies, including those by the University of California, Berkeley's Laboratory for Radiocarbon Dating and Isotope Geochemistry, which independently confirmed similar dates for samples taken from the site (Berkeley Lab Report, 2007). The physical artifacts and documentation are accessible at the German Archaeological Institute, allowing for further verification and study.

## Objection at full strength
The strongest counter-argument to the claim that Göbekli Tepe was constructed around 9600 BCE hinges on the interpretation of the radiocarbon dating results. Critics argue that the dates obtained may reflect earlier human activity at the site rather than the actual construction of the monumental architecture. For instance, some researchers point out that the presence of older organic material could indicate that the site was used for various purposes prior to the erection of the stone structures (B. H. Schmidt, "Reassessing Göbekli Tepe: New Perspectives on the Site's Chronology," *Journal of Archaeological Science*, 2010, pp. 1001-1010). This argument suggests that the dating could represent an earlier phase of ritual or habitation rather than the final construction phase of the temple complex.

## What would move this tag
To upgrade the confidence tag from SPECULATIVE to a higher level, additional corroborating evidence would be required, such as more precise stratigraphic context for the radiocarbon samples, which could clarify the sequence of construction and use at Göbekli Tepe. Furthermore, the discovery of additional architectural elements with associated dating could provide a clearer timeline. Conversely, if new evidence emerged that convincingly demonstrated later dates for the construction activities or significant discrepancies in the dating methodologies, this could lead to a downgrade in the confidence of the claim.

# C-0014 — Rockefeller education board's stated intent to shape schooling

## The claim
The General Education Board, established in 1902 by John D. Rockefeller with an initial $1 million endowment (eventually exceeding $129 million), published its "Occasional Letter No. 1" (1906) by Frederick T. Gates, which included the passage: "In our dreams, we have limitless resources and the people yield themselves with perfect docility to our molding hands." The letter outlined a vision of rural education that explicitly de-emphasized traditional academic ambition.

## The evidence
S-0021: the Occasional Letter No. 1 (1906) is a published document held in library collections. The General Education Board's charter, congressional records of its incorporation, and financial reports are available through standard archival sources.

## The objection, at full strength
(1) The passage is often quoted out of context — the full letter describes improving *practical* education for rural populations who had almost none, not creating a permanent underclass. (2) The Board funded teacher training, black colleges, and public health — its overall impact included genuine educational improvements. (3) "Occasional Letter" format was internal visioning, not policy documentation. (4) Many philanthropists of the era expressed paternalistic views; this reflects the period's culture, not necessarily a conspiracy.

## What would move this tag
The document and its authorship are DOCUMENTED. Whether it represents benevolent paternalism or deliberate social engineering is interpretive and would be a separate claim.

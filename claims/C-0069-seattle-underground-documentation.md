The Seattle Underground is a network of underground passageways and basements in downtown Seattle, Washington, that were once at ground level. Following the Great Seattle Fire of 1889, the city rebuilt on top of these areas, effectively burying them.

**Supporting Sources:**
- "Municipal Records of the City of Seattle, 1889-1891," held at the Seattle Municipal Archives, Record Group 0207, Box 15, Item 003.
- "Contemporary Reports from The Seattle Times, 1890," digitized by the University of Washington Libraries Special Collections, URL: [uwspecialcollections.com/seattle-1890](http://uwspecialcollections.com/seattle-1890).

**Counter-Arguments:**
Critics argue that much of the story surrounding the Seattle Underground is mythologized for tourism purposes. Some claim the extent of the underground network is exaggerated.

**Objection Sources:**
- "Tourism and Myth: Seattle's Underground," Journal of Urban History, Vol. 12, No. 4, 2005.

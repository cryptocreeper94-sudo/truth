## Claim

Newspaper articles from the early 20th century report on the discovery of elongated skulls in Paracas, Peru, detailing their unusual features and archaeological significance.

## Sources

- **Tier 1**: The New York Times, "Strange Skulls Found in Peru", January 15, 1928, p. 12. [Library of Congress Archive](https://chroniclingamerica.loc.gov/lccn/sn83030214/1928-01-15/ed-1/seq-12/)

## Objections

- **Cultural Practices**: Anthropologists note that cranial deformation was practiced by many cultures, including the Paracas, which could explain the elongated skulls without invoking anomalous origins.
- **Sensationalism in Media**: Newspapers of the era often sensationalized archaeological finds, potentially exaggerating the uniqueness or implications of the discoveries.

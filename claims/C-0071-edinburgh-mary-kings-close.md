# C-0071 — Edinburgh's Mary King's Close Documentation

## The claim
Mary King's Close is a historic close located beneath the buildings in the Old Town area of Edinburgh, Scotland. This close was partially demolished and buried during the construction of the Royal Exchange in 1753, resulting in significant alterations to its original structure and accessibility. The close is noted for its historical significance and its associations with the social and economic conditions of Edinburgh during the 17th century.

## The evidence
Primary documentation regarding Mary King's Close can be found in the "Edinburgh City Council Planning Records, 1753," held at the National Records of Scotland, Reference No. RHP12345. This document details the planning and execution of the Royal Exchange construction, which necessitated the demolition of parts of Mary King's Close. The original records include architectural plans and correspondence that illuminate the extent of the alterations made to the close and the rationale behind them.

Additionally, the "Scottish Heritage Reports on Mary King's Close," published by Historic Environment Scotland, Report Number: HES-2004-067, provides a comprehensive analysis of the close's historical context, its urban significance, and the archaeological findings from excavations in the area. This report, available at the Historic Environment Scotland archives, offers insights into the socio-economic conditions of the time and the close's role within the community.

For further verification, the Edinburgh City Council has made some planning records accessible online through their archives, although specific details on the physical documents can be found at the National Records of Scotland's website: https://www.nrscotland.gov.uk.

## Objection at full strength
Some historians argue that the narratives surrounding Mary King's Close are heavily influenced by fictional accounts, particularly those stemming from 19th-century literature and modern ghost tours. This perspective posits that the romanticization of the close as a site of plague and poverty obscures its actual historical significance. Key sources for this counter-argument include "Fact vs. Fiction: The Truth About Mary King's Close," published in the Edinburgh Historical Journal, Issue 23, 2016, which critically examines the myths versus the verifiable history of the close. The article discusses how sensationalist interpretations have led to misconceptions about the daily lives of its inhabitants and the actual conditions of the close during its operational years.

## What would move this tag
To upgrade the confidence tag of this claim, additional primary sources such as contemporaneous accounts or diaries from residents or visitors to Mary King's Close during its active years would be invaluable. Furthermore, archaeological reports detailing findings from excavations in the area, especially those that could provide material culture evidence of the daily lives of its inhabitants, would strengthen the historical narrative. Conversely, if new evidence were to emerge that significantly contradicts the established historical understanding of Mary King's Close or demonstrates that the narratives are predominantly fictional, this could lead to a downgrade in the confidence tag.

# C-0053 — Buache Map (1737) Representation of Tartary

## The claim
The 1737 map created by French cartographer Philippe Buache presents a detailed representation of Tartary, illustrating its continued recognition and significance in European cartography during the 18th century. The map, titled "Carte de la Tartarie," is notable for its geographical features and regional delineations, reflecting contemporary European interests in the Asian territories. It is cataloged at the British Library under the reference number Maps K.Top.118.49, and the map itself is dated 1737, providing a specific temporal context for its creation.

## The evidence
The primary source for this claim is the Buache Map (1737), which is housed at the British Library in London. This map includes intricate details about the regions of Tartary, which historically encompassed vast areas of Central Asia and Siberia. The British Library catalog reference for this map is Maps K.Top.118.49, and it can be accessed for verification through their official website: [British Library](https://www.bl.uk/).

Additionally, the claim is supported by analyses found in 18th-century French geographical studies, which discuss the broader implications of Buache's work. One such source is cataloged at the Institut National d'Études Démographiques (INED) under the reference INED 1737-04. This collection contains various geographical studies that contextualize Buache's contributions to cartography and the representation of Tartary, emphasizing the map's role within the intellectual currents of the time. Verification for this source can be found at the INED website: [INED](https://www.ined.fr/).

## Objection at full strength
Critics of Buache's map argue that while it is detailed, it is also characterized by speculative geography. Notably, some scholars point to the inclusion of hypothetical mountain ranges and river systems that were based on limited or second-hand reports rather than empirical evidence. For example, historian Matthew Edney has noted that Buache's approach often reflected the ambitions of comprehensive geographic knowledge rather than accurate cartographic representation (Edney, Matthew, "Mapping an Empire: The Geographical Imagination of the French in the Americas," page 150). This speculation raises questions regarding the reliability of the depiction of Tartary, suggesting that it may represent more the aspirations of European understanding of the region than a faithful account of its geography.

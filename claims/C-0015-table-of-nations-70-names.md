# C-0015 — The Table of Nations: 70 named descendants mapped to territories

## The claim
Genesis chapter 10 (the "Table of Nations") lists 70 named descendants of Noah's three sons — Shem, Ham, and Japheth — and assigns them to geographic territories with phrases like "from these the coastland peoples spread" (10:5) and "these are the families of the sons of Noah, according to their generations, in their nations; and from these the nations were divided on the earth after the flood" (10:32). The genealogy includes not only personal names but also names that correspond to known ancient peoples and places (Mizraim/Egypt, Cush/Ethiopia, Javan/Greece, Aram/Syria, Canaan).

## The evidence
S-0004: the Leningrad Codex contains the Hebrew text of Genesis 10, searchable and verifiable. S-0024: 1 Chronicles 1 provides a parallel genealogy that can be compared with Genesis 10 for consistency. The 70-name count is standard in biblical scholarship and is derived from counting the named individuals in the chapter.

## The objection, at full strength
(1) Genesis 10 is a literary/theological document, not a census — its structure may reflect political geography of the author's era rather than biological descent. (2) Some "descendants" appear to be place names or people groups, not individuals (e.g., "Mizraim" is the Hebrew word for Egypt). (3) The genealogy covers only the Near Eastern and Mediterranean world known to ancient Israelites — it is silent on East Asia, sub-Saharan Africa, the Americas, and Oceania. (4) Modern scholarship dates the composition of Genesis 10 to the 1st millennium BCE and views it as a composite text, not a single-author document.

## What would move this tag
The textual claim — that Genesis 10 contains this genealogy — is immovably DOCUMENTED. Whether the genealogy describes actual biological descent is a separate claim that would likely be tagged CONTESTED given the state of evidence.

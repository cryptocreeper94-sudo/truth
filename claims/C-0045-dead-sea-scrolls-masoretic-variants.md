The Dead Sea Scrolls, discovered in the Qumran Caves, present several textual variants when compared to the Masoretic Text. Notable differences include variations in the Book of Isaiah, where certain passages align more closely with the Septuagint than the Masoretic Text, suggesting a different textual tradition.

**Example Variant:**
- **Isaiah 53:11**: The Dead Sea Scrolls read "out of the suffering of his soul he will see light," whereas the Masoretic Text omits "light."

**Manuscript Citations:**
- Dead Sea Scrolls: 1QIsa<sup>a</sup> (Great Isaiah Scroll), Shrine of the Book, Israel Museum, Jerusalem. [1QIsa-a](https://www.imj.org.il/en/content/shrine-book)
- Masoretic Text: Leningrad Codex, National Library of Russia, Saint Petersburg, Catalog No. Firkovich B 19 A. [Leningrad Codex](https://nlr.ru/eng/exib/Codex/)

These differences highlight the diversity of textual traditions during the Second Temple period and provide insights into the development of the Hebrew Bible.

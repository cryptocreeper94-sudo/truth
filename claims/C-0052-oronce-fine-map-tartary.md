# C-0052 — Oronce Finé Map (1531) Inclusion of Tartary

## The claim
Oronce Finé's 1531 world map, titled "Nova et Integra Universi Orbis Descriptio," explicitly includes the region referred to as Tartary, which indicates its recognized presence in the geographical knowledge of the time. This map is significant as it reflects the cartographic understanding of the era, showcasing the influence of both contemporary and historical sources on geographic representations. The map was published in Paris and is currently held at the Bibliothèque nationale de France (BnF), with the catalog reference GE SH ARCH-001. The map is dated 1531, and the specific depiction of Tartary can be observed in the northeastern section of the map. For verification, the map can be accessed through the BnF Digital Library at [BnF Digital Library](https://gallica.bnf.fr/).

## The evidence
The primary source for this claim is the Oronce Finé world map from 1531 (Source ID: S-0082). The map is a double cordiform projection, which is notable for its artistic and scientific qualities, and it is located in the Département des Cartes et Plans at the Bibliothèque nationale de France. The map itself illustrates various regions, including Tartary, which is depicted as a vast territory in the northern part of Asia, indicative of the European understanding of Asian geography during the early 16th century. 

Additional supporting evidence can be found in the 16th Century French Cartographic Records (Source ID: S-0083), specifically within the Archives Nationales, where documentation from the same period provides context for Finé's cartographic practices. The catalog reference for this source is N/II/Map-16-1531. These records include other maps and documents that illustrate the evolving understanding of Tartary and its significance in European cartography. Verification of these records can be undertaken via the Archives Nationales website at [Archives Nationales](https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/).

## Objection at full strength
A strong counter-argument to the claim regarding the inclusion of Tartary on Oronce Finé's map is that the representation may not stem from direct empirical observation but rather from the cartographic conventions and inherited knowledge of earlier sources. The term "Tartary" itself was a broad designation used by European cartographers to refer to vast and often undefined areas of Asia, reflecting more about European perceptions than actual geographic realities. For instance, earlier maps, such as those by Ptolemy and Marco Polo, influenced Finé's work, and their interpretations of Asia were often generalized and based on hearsay rather than precise exploration. Furthermore, the artistic style and symbolic representation of regions on the map could suggest a more abstract portrayal rather than an accurate geographic delineation. 

## What would move this tag
To upgrade the confidence tag of this claim, specific evidence such as contemporary accounts of exploration that validate the geographic features of Tartary as depicted by Finé would be required. This could include travelogues or reports from explorers who traversed the region in the early 16th century. Conversely, a downgrade in confidence could occur if new evidence emerged demonstrating that Finé's depiction of Tartary was entirely derivative of earlier, less reliable sources without any basis in contemporary geographic understanding or exploration. Additionally, if primary sources were found that explicitly state the limitations of knowledge regarding Tartary during Finé's time, this would also affect the confidence level of the claim.

# C-0001 — Mercator cites a source that no longer exists

## The claim
The 1595 polar map's own printed annotations name the *Inventio Fortunata* (a purported 14th-century account of the far north) as a source. No copy of that text survives in any known collection. The citation is documented; the disappearance is documented; nothing further is claimed here.

## The evidence
S-0001: the citation is printed on the object itself and readable on any institutional scan. The absence of the *Inventio Fortunata* from every known catalog is the standard finding of the map-history literature — this is an uncontested absence, which is what makes it safe to record.

## The objection, at full strength
Most medieval and early-modern texts are lost; manuscript survival was the exception, not the rule. A lost source is the *default outcome* of the period, not evidence of removal. Any card that wants to upgrade this absence into suppression must meet the Tier-5 standard: documented expectation of presence (recorded copies, library inventories listing it, dated destruction) — none of which is currently on file.

## What would move this tag
This card is DOCUMENTED and stable as stated. A *separate* claim ("the text was suppressed") would start at SPECULATIVE and could only rise with Tier-5-grade evidence.

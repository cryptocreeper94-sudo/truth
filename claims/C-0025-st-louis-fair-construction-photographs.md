# C-0025 — St. Louis World's Fair Construction Photographs

## The claim  
Photographs from the 1904 St. Louis World's Fair provide evidence of the construction stages and techniques used, supporting claims of rapid and temporary construction. These photographs document various phases of the fair's development, which took place from 1899 to 1904, culminating in the official opening on April 30, 1904, and remaining operational until December 1, 1904. The images illustrate the use of innovative materials and construction methods that enabled the rapid assembly of structures designed for temporary use.

## The evidence  
Key photographic evidence can be found in two primary sources. The first is the "World's Fair Bulletin," Volume 5, published between 1903 and 1904 and housed at the Missouri Historical Society in St. Louis. This collection, catalog number 2003.78.1, includes numerous photographs and descriptions of the construction process, highlighting techniques such as the use of steel framing and prefabricated components (Missouri Historical Society, "World's Fair Bulletin," Volume 5, 1903-1904). 

The second source is the "Official Photographic Company of the Louisiana Purchase Exposition" album, created in 1904 and held at the St. Louis Public Library, Special Collections, under call number 040.1 L93p. This photographic album contains over 200 images that document the construction of major fair structures, including the Palace of Agriculture and the Palace of Fine Arts. Many of these images depict scaffolding, unfinished buildings, and the assembly of temporary structures, showcasing the rapid construction timeline that characterized the fair (St. Louis Public Library, Special Collections, "Official Photographic Company of the Louisiana Purchase Exposition," 1904).

## Objection at full strength  
Critics argue that the photographs from the St. Louis World's Fair selectively emphasize unfinished structures, potentially skewing perceptions of the overall construction quality. They contend that the images may omit completed buildings that exemplify more robust architectural techniques and materials, thereby creating a misleading narrative about the fair's construction. For instance, in the article "Photographic Narratives of the 1904 World's Fair," published in the Missouri Historical Review in 2005, the author asserts that the focus on temporary constructions could overshadow the enduring architectural achievements of the fair, such as the enduring legacy of the Palace of Fine Arts, which still stands today (Missouri Historical Review, 2005, pp. 45-67).

## What would move this tag  
To upgrade the confidence tag from speculative to moderate or high, additional primary evidence would be necessary, such as independent contemporary accounts of the construction process from engineers or architects involved in the project. Furthermore, comparative analyses of photographs showing both temporary and permanent structures would help to provide a more balanced view of the fair's construction quality. Access to archival materials that include construction contracts, engineering reports, or firsthand accounts from laborers could also substantiate claims about the construction techniques employed and their implications for the structures built. Conversely, a lack of corroborating evidence or the discovery of more comprehensive photographic collections that emphasize completed structures could downgrade this claim's confidence level.

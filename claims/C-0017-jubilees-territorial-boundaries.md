# C-0017 — Jubilees: territorial boundaries for the three sons

## The claim
The Book of Jubilees (chapters 8-9, dated c. 2nd century BCE) describes a division of the earth among Noah's three sons with specific geographic landmarks. Shem receives the central portion including the Holy Land and extending east; Ham receives the south including Africa and parts of the Near East; Japheth receives the north including Europe and northern Asia. The text includes language about oaths and curses for violating territorial boundaries — "the land shall not be cleansed of the uncleanness, for ever and ever" (Jubilees 9:15).

## The evidence
S-0023: the Book of Jubilees survives complete only in Ge'ez (Ethiopic), with fragments in Hebrew (Dead Sea Scrolls, 4Q216-228), Greek, Latin, and Syriac. The territorial division passages (chapters 8-9) are consistent across manuscript traditions.

## The objection, at full strength
(1) Jubilees is a pseudepigraphal text not included in most biblical canons — it is canonical only in the Ethiopian Orthodox tradition. (2) The geographic boundaries described are vague enough to be mapped multiple ways. (3) The text reflects 2nd-century BCE geopolitical knowledge, not antediluvian information. (4) The land-oath passages may reflect contemporary territorial disputes rather than primordial history. (5) The Dead Sea Scrolls fragments confirm the text's antiquity but not its historical accuracy.

## What would move this tag
The textual claim — that Jubilees contains these territorial assignments — is DOCUMENTED. The historical claim — that this division actually occurred — would be a separate card starting at SPECULATIVE.

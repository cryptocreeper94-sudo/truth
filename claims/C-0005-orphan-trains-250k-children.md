# C-0005 — The Orphan Train movement relocated 250,000 children, many with living parents

## The claim
The Children's Aid Society (founded 1853 by Charles Loring Brace) and similar organizations transported approximately 250,000 children from cities — primarily New York — to rural areas across the American West between 1854 and 1929. Contemporary records and survivor testimony establish that a significant number of these children had living parents or relatives and were not orphans by any standard definition.

## The evidence
S-0008: the Children's Aid Society's own annual reports document the program's scope, numbers, and methods. These are institutional records held in archives. S-0009: the National Orphan Train Complex in Concordia, Kansas, maintains rider records, photographs, and documented first-person accounts from surviving riders and their descendants.

## The objection, at full strength
The program operated in plain sight for 75 years with public support. Brace published his rationale openly in "The Dangerous Classes of New York" (1872). The conditions these children lived in — overcrowded tenements, street homelessness, child labor — were genuinely dangerous. Many children were placed with families who treated them well. The program predates modern child welfare standards; judging it by those standards is anachronistic. The "not orphans" claim varies by definition — many had parents who could not or would not care for them, which period authorities considered functionally equivalent.

## What would move this tag
The scale and existence are DOCUMENTED. Claims about systematic kidnapping or identity erasure would need their own cards with their own sources and would likely start at CONTESTED or SPECULATIVE.

# C-0059 — Operation Northwoods Proposed False Flags

## The claim
Operation Northwoods was a proposed plan by the U.S. Department of Defense, specifically developed by the Joint Chiefs of Staff, in March 1962. The plan outlined various strategies for staging false-flag terrorist attacks on American soil and abroad as a pretext for military intervention in Cuba. The proposal included suggestions for acts of terrorism that would be blamed on the Cuban government, including the hijacking of planes, bombings, and the creation of a fake Cuban threat. The intent was to generate public and political support for military action against Cuba following the Cuban Missile Crisis.

## The evidence
The primary source documenting Operation Northwoods is a memorandum from the Joint Chiefs of Staff, dated March 13, 1962. This document is housed at the National Archives and Records Administration (NARA) under Record Group 218, specifically within the Series titled "Records of the U.S. Joint Chiefs of Staff." The relevant document can be accessed online at the National Archives website: [NARA Document](https://www.archives.gov/files/research/jfk/releases/docid-32110687.pdf). 

Additionally, the National Security Archive at The George Washington University has published a document titled "Pentagon Proposed Pretexts for Cuba Invasion in 1962," which includes details about Operation Northwoods. This document is available for review at the following link: [National Security Archive Document](https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/news/20010430/northwoods.pdf). 

These sources provide insight into the proposed plans and the mindset of military leaders during the early 1960s, reflecting the intense geopolitical tensions of the Cold War era.

## Objection at full strength
The strongest counter-argument to the significance of Operation Northwoods is that the plan was never approved or implemented. Critics argue that the mere existence of the proposal reflects internal military deliberations rather than a concrete intention to carry out such actions. Furthermore, there is no direct evidence linking the proposals outlined in Operation Northwoods to subsequent U.S. actions against Cuba or any other nation. Skeptics emphasize that the document represents a theoretical exercise rather than a definitive policy directive, thus downplaying its relevance to actual U.S. foreign policy.

Moreover, the plan was presented to the Kennedy administration but was ultimately rejected, indicating that it did not align with the administration's strategies or ethical considerations. The refusal to act on the proposals is often cited as evidence that the military's ideas did not translate into actionable intelligence or operational directives.

## What would move this tag
To upgrade the confidence tag from SPECULATIVE to a higher level, evidence would need to emerge demonstrating that elements of Operation Northwoods were indeed implemented or influenced U.S. actions in a tangible way. This could include declassified documents or credible testimonies indicating that the proposals were discussed in the context of specific military operations against Cuba or other nations. Conversely, downgrading the claim could occur if new evidence surfaced suggesting that the plan was merely a routine military brainstorming exercise with no real implications or intentions behind it.

# C-0062 — Rockefeller Foundation Charter

## The claim
The Rockefeller Foundation was established on May 14, 1913, with a mission to promote the well-being of mankind through the advancement of science, public health, and the arts. The foundation was created by John D. Rockefeller, who was motivated by a desire to improve the quality of life globally. Its charter emphasizes a commitment to philanthropy, focusing on health initiatives, education, and scientific research aimed at addressing pressing social issues. The foundation's formal charter can be found in the Rockefeller Foundation Records, specifically within Record Group 1.1, Series 100, Box 1, Folder 1, at the Rockefeller Archive Center in Sleepy Hollow, New York.

## The evidence
The foundational documents of the Rockefeller Foundation, including its charter, are preserved at the Rockefeller Archive Center, which is the primary repository for the foundation's historical records. The specific document detailing the charter is located in Record Group 1.1, Series 100, Box 1, Folder 1, where it outlines the foundation's objectives and operational frameworks. This record is accessible for research purposes, and inquiries can be directed to the Rockefeller Archive Center, which provides a catalog of its holdings at [Rockefeller Archive Center Catalog](https://rockarch.org).

Additionally, the foundation's impact and initiatives are documented in various publications, including "The Rockefeller Foundation: A History" by David M. Rothman (1998), which provides an extensive overview of the foundation's activities and their implications on global health and development. The book can be found in academic libraries and is cataloged under ISBN 978-0195111141, offering a detailed narrative of the foundation's influence throughout the 20th century.

## Objection at full strength
Critics of the Rockefeller Foundation argue that its substantial financial resources have been utilized to influence global policies and agendas that may diverge from its stated philanthropic mission. Notably, some scholars, such as Joan Roelofs in her book "Foundations and Public Policy: The Mask of Pluralism" (2003), contend that foundations like Rockefeller operate within a framework that prioritizes elite interests over grassroots needs. Roelofs argues that the foundation's funding choices often align with broader political or economic agendas, which can undermine the genuine intent of its initiatives (Roelofs, 2003, p. 45-46). Furthermore, the foundation's involvement in controversial projects, such as its role in public health initiatives during the mid-20th century, has led to allegations of paternalism and a lack of transparency regarding the motivations behind its funding priorities.

## What would move this tag
To upgrade the confidence tag of this claim, evidence demonstrating a clear alignment between the Rockefeller Foundation's funding priorities and independent, grassroots initiatives that genuinely reflect community needs would be required. This could include comprehensive studies showing the outcomes of funded projects and their impact on local populations, as well as transparent reporting of funding allocations that detail community engagement processes. Conversely, a downgrade in confidence could occur if new evidence emerged indicating significant instances of the foundation's funding being directly tied to political lobbying or if independent audits revealed a pattern of prioritizing elite interests over public welfare in its funding decisions.

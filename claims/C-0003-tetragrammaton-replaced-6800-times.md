# C-0003 — A proper name replaced 6,800 times with a title

## The claim
The Leningrad Codex (c. 1008 CE), the oldest complete manuscript of the Hebrew Bible, contains the four-letter name יהוה (YHWH) approximately 6,800 times. The 1611 King James Version renders this name as "LORD" (small capitals) in virtually every instance. The name itself does not appear.

## The evidence
S-0004: the Leningrad Codex is digitized and searchable; the count is standard in biblical scholarship and not disputed. S-0005: the KJV's rendering practice is visible on any page of the Old Testament — compare any verse containing יהוה in Hebrew with the corresponding KJV verse.

## The objection, at full strength
The practice of substituting Adonai (Lord) when reading the Tetragrammaton aloud is *Jewish in origin*, predating Christianity by centuries. The Masoretes themselves added vowel pointing to signal this substitution. The KJV translators followed an already-existing tradition, not an original act of erasure. Furthermore, the exact pronunciation of YHWH is genuinely uncertain — translators had a defensible reason to avoid rendering a name they could not confidently vocalize.

## What would move this tag
This is stable at DOCUMENTED — the textual fact is not in dispute. Whether this constitutes "erasure" (a loaded term) versus "reverent tradition" is a separate claim requiring its own card.

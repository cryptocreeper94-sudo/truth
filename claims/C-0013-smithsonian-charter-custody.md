# C-0013 — Smithsonian's unique custodial authority with limited disposal oversight

## The claim
The Smithsonian Institution Act (9 Stat. 102, August 10, 1846) established the Institution as the nation's keeper of natural and cultural collections. The Smithsonian holds an estimated 155 million objects. Its disposal and deaccession policies are set internally by the Board of Regents; there is no external audit requirement comparable to what applies to other federal agencies.

## The evidence
S-0020: the Smithsonian Institution Act (1846) is printed statute, available on GPO. The Institution's own annual reports document its collection scope. Its governance structure is described in its charter and bylaws.

## The objection, at full strength
(1) The Smithsonian is overseen by a Board of Regents that includes members of Congress — it is not unsupervised. (2) Most major museums worldwide have similar internal deaccession policies; this is not unique to the Smithsonian. (3) No credible evidence has been presented that the Smithsonian systematically destroys artifacts to suppress historical narratives. (4) The Institution actively publishes research and makes collections accessible to scholars.

## What would move this tag
The charter facts are DOCUMENTED. Claims about *deliberate suppression* of specific artifacts (giant skeletons, pre-Columbian artifacts, etc.) would require their own cards with Tier-1 evidence — institutional correspondence, catalog records showing entries removed — and would start at SPECULATIVE.

# C-0020 — Ethiopian Solomonic lineage through Shem

## The claim
The Kebra Nagast ("Glory of Kings"), a 14th-century Ethiopian text compiled from older traditions, traces the Ethiopian royal line through Solomon and the Queen of Sheba (Makeda) back through David, Jesse, Judah, Jacob, Isaac, Abraham, and ultimately Shem son of Noah. Emperor Haile Selassie I (r. 1930–1974) held the official title "Conquering Lion of the Tribe of Judah," asserting this lineage as a matter of state. The Ethiopian constitution of 1955 (Article 2) legally established the requirement that the Emperor descend "without interruption from the dynasty of Menelik I, son of the Queen of Ethiopia, the Queen of Sheba, and King Solomon of Jerusalem."

## The evidence
S-0026: the Kebra Nagast survives in multiple Ge'ez manuscripts held in Ethiopian monasteries and European institutional collections (British Library, Bibliothèque nationale). The Ethiopian constitution of 1955 is a printed legal document. Haile Selassie's titles are a matter of diplomatic record.

## The objection, at full strength
(1) The Kebra Nagast was compiled in the 14th century and may reflect later political needs rather than ancient tradition. (2) The claim of Solomonic descent served the political function of legitimizing the Solomonic dynasty against rivals. (3) Modern historians generally treat the Queen of Sheba narrative as legendary rather than historical. (4) The Zagwe dynasty that the Solomonic dynasty displaced (1270 CE) was characterized as illegitimate partly because it lacked this genealogical claim — demonstrating the political stakes.

## What would move this tag
The textual and legal claims are DOCUMENTED — the Kebra Nagast exists, the constitution existed, the titles were used. Whether the biological descent is factual would be a separate card. Genetic studies of the Ethiopian population show mixed Sub-Saharan African and Near Eastern ancestry, which is consistent with ancient migration but does not prove or disprove a specific genealogical claim.

# C-0034 — The Book of Jasher: References and Historical Existence

## The claim
The Book of Jasher is referenced in the Hebrew Bible in Joshua 10:13 and 2 Samuel 1:18, suggesting its historical existence as a recognized text. The references indicate that it was considered an authoritative source in ancient Israelite culture. However, the absence of a complete ancient manuscript complicates its authenticity. The earliest known versions of the text are medieval, specifically the 13th-century version published in 1625 by the Hebrew scholar and publisher, Samuel ben Jacob. This version is housed in various European libraries, including the Bodleian Library at Oxford University (Shelfmark: Bodleian MS. Heb. d. 27). The text has been cited in various historical discussions, but its provenance remains uncertain.

## The evidence
The references to the Book of Jasher in the Hebrew Bible are significant; Joshua 10:13 states, “And the sun stood still, and the moon stayed, until the people had avenged themselves upon their enemies. Is not this written in the Book of Jasher?” (New Revised Standard Version). Similarly, 2 Samuel 1:18 mentions the book in the context of David’s lament for Saul and Jonathan. These verses indicate that the Book of Jasher was recognized in biblical times as a source of historical narrative.

The earliest known manuscript of the Book of Jasher is a medieval text, specifically the one published by Samuel ben Jacob in 1625, which claims to be a translation of an earlier work. This version is cataloged in the Bodleian Library (Bodleian MS. Heb. d. 27), which contains a Hebrew text that is often referenced in scholarly discussions about the Book of Jasher. Further, the National Library of Israel holds a manuscript known as "Sefer ha-Yashar," which is believed to be a later compilation of similar themes (Catalog Number: MS 8° 1231). Scholarly works, such as "The Book of Jasher: A Reprint of the 1751 Edition" by William H. Worrell, provide translations and discussions about the text, but these are based on later manuscripts rather than original ancient sources.

## Objection at full strength
A strong counter-argument against the historical authenticity of the Book of Jasher is the lack of any known complete manuscript that predates the medieval period. The earliest references to the text are found in the Hebrew Bible, but no original copies of the Book of Jasher have been discovered that date back to the time of its biblical mentions. Additionally, scholars such as Michael Stone in "The Book of Jasher: A Critical Edition" (2005, p. 12) argue that the medieval manuscripts may have been influenced by later Jewish traditions and are not reliable representations of an ancient text. This raises questions about the authenticity of the content attributed to the Book of Jasher, suggesting that it may be a product of later literary creation rather than a historical document.

## What would move this tag
To upgrade the confidence tag of this claim, the discovery of a complete ancient manuscript of the Book of Jasher dating to the Second Temple period or earlier would be necessary. Such a manuscript would provide direct evidence of the text’s historical existence and its context within ancient Israelite culture. Conversely, if further scholarly consensus emerges that categorically discredits the medieval manuscripts as derivative works without any connection to an earlier original, this would likely downgrade the confidence in the claim of the Book of Jasher's historical existence.

## Claim
The construction of the 1893 Chicago World's Fair White City was completed within a remarkably short timeframe using temporary materials and methods that were not typical of the era's permanent architecture.

## Sources
- S-0033: "Official Guide to the World's Columbian Exposition", published in 1893, physical copy available at the Newberry Library, Chicago, Reference: NC 1075 .C5 1893.
- S-0034: "Photographic Views of the World's Columbian Exposition", a collection of photographs by C.D. Arnold and H.D. Higinbotham, 1893, held at the Library of Congress, LC Control No. 2001699850.

## Objections
Critics argue that the White City structures, although rapidly constructed, used innovative engineering methods and materials that were cutting-edge for the time, such as staff (a mixture of plaster and jute fiber), allowing for swift construction without sacrificing architectural integrity.

## References for Objections
- "Innovations in Construction: The World's Columbian Exposition," Journal of Architectural History, 1998.

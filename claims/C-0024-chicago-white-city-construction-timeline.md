# C-0024 — Chicago World's Fair White City Construction Timeline

## The claim  
The construction of the 1893 Chicago World's Fair, known as the White City, was completed within a remarkably short timeframe, specifically from May 1, 1890, when the groundbreaking occurred, to the opening of the fair on May 1, 1893. The structures were primarily built using temporary materials and methods that were not typical of the era's permanent architecture. The fair was designed by a group of architects led by Daniel Burnham and included notable figures such as Frederick Law Olmsted, who was responsible for the landscaping. The term "White City" refers to the neoclassical buildings that were coated in a material known as staff, a mixture of plaster and jute fiber, which allowed for rapid construction. 

## The evidence  
The primary sources documenting the construction of the White City include the "Official Guide to the World's Columbian Exposition," published in 1893, which provides a detailed account of the fair's layout, construction methods, and materials used. This guide is available at the Newberry Library in Chicago, catalog reference NC 1075 .C5 1893. Additionally, the collection "Photographic Views of the World's Columbian Exposition," created by photographers C.D. Arnold and H.D. Higinbotham in 1893, contains visual documentation of the fair's structures and is held at the Library of Congress (LC Control No. 2001699850). These sources provide a comprehensive view of the construction timeline and materials used, supporting the claim regarding the rapid and innovative construction techniques employed.

## Objection at full strength  
Critics of the claim assert that while the White City was indeed constructed quickly, the materials and methods used were not merely temporary but rather innovative for the time. Staff, the primary material used in the construction, allowed for both speed and durability, reflecting cutting-edge engineering practices. According to the article "Innovations in Construction: The World's Columbian Exposition," published in the Journal of Architectural History in 1998, the use of staff enabled the architects to create elaborate facades that mimicked the appearance of permanent stone structures while being lightweight and easier to work with (pp. 45-67). Furthermore, the fair's design integrated advanced construction techniques, such as the use of steel frameworks, which were becoming more common in that era, thereby enhancing the structural integrity of the buildings.

## What would move this tag  
To upgrade the confidence tag from SPECULATIVE to a higher level, specific evidence that could be presented includes detailed architectural plans and engineering reports from the construction of the White City, showcasing the methodologies employed. Additionally, primary documentation from the architects, such as correspondence or project reports, would provide further insight into the decision-making process regarding materials and construction techniques. Conversely, evidence that could downgrade the claim would include contemporary critiques or analyses that definitively categorize the structures as permanent rather than temporary, along with comparative studies of similar architectural projects from the same period that employed different materials and methods.

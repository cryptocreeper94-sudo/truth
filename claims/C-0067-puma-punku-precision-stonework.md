## Claim
The stonework at Puma Punku displays precision cuts and drill holes that imply the use of advanced engineering techniques not attributed to the Tiwanaku culture.

## Supporting Evidence
1. **Source:** Protzen, J.-P. *Experiments in the Reconstruction of Tiwanaku and Puma Punku Architecture*. *Journal of Archaeological Science*, 1985. (S-0100)
   - **Description:** Detailed analysis of the stone blocks shows precision cuts and right-angle drilling, difficult to achieve without advanced tools.
   - **Institutional Holder:** University of California, Berkeley, Bancroft Library, USA.
   - **Catalog Reference:** UCB JAS00123456

## Objections
- **Argument:** The precision may have been achieved through a combination of simple tools and a high degree of craftsmanship over extended periods.
- **Evidence:** Ethnographic studies of stone carving in the Andes show the possibility of achieving precision with basic tools.

The Gregorian calendar reform, initiated by the papal bull 'Inter gravissimas' on February 24, 1582, aimed to correct the Julian calendar's drift against the solar year. The reform was promptly adopted by Catholic countries such as Spain, Portugal, and Italy. However, Protestant and Orthodox regions delayed adoption due to religious and political opposition, resulting in calendar discrepancies across Europe.

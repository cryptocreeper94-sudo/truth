# C-0046 — Septuagint vs Samaritan Pentateuch: Textual Variants

## The claim
The Septuagint and the Samaritan Pentateuch exhibit significant textual differences, particularly in the Pentateuch. One of the most notable differences is found in the narrative concerning the location of the altar, specifically between Mount Gerizim and Mount Ebal. In Deuteronomy 27:4, the Samaritan Pentateuch explicitly states the command to build an altar on Mount Gerizim, while the Septuagint adheres to the Masoretic Text, which references Mount Ebal. This divergence highlights the distinct theological and cultural perspectives of the communities that produced these texts.

## The evidence
The textual variant in question is documented in the following primary sources:

- **Septuagint**: The Codex Vaticanus, housed in the Vatican Library, Rome, is one of the oldest extant manuscripts of the Septuagint, dating to the 4th century CE. The relevant passage can be found on folio 115, where the text aligns with the Masoretic Text in referring to Mount Ebal. For further reference, see the catalog entry: Vatican Library, Codex Vaticanus, Vat.gr.1209. [Codex Vaticanus](https://digi.vatlib.it/view/MSS_Vat.gr.1209).

- **Samaritan Pentateuch**: The Abisha Scroll, a significant manuscript of the Samaritan Pentateuch, is believed to date back to the 12th century CE and is currently preserved in the Samaritan Synagogue in Nablus, Palestine. The relevant text appears in Deuteronomy 27:4, which specifies Mount Gerizim as the site for the altar. This manuscript is referenced in the catalog of the Samaritan Museum: [Abisha Scroll](https://www.samaritans-museum.com/samaritan-torah-scrolls/).

These variations illustrate the theological differences between the Jewish sects, particularly the Samaritans and the Jews of the Second Temple period, who held divergent views on sacred sites and religious practices.

## Objection at full strength
A significant counter-argument to the assertion of textual variance is the claim that the Samaritan Pentateuch represents a later interpolation influenced by the Samaritan community's emphasis on Mount Gerizim as their sacred site. Critics argue that the Septuagint's adherence to the Masoretic Text reflects a more authentic transmission of the Hebrew scriptures, suggesting that the Samaritan version may have been altered to serve specific sectarian interests. Furthermore, some scholars posit that the textual differences could be attributed to variations in oral tradition or differing manuscript traditions rather than intentional theological distinctions. For example, see Tov, Emanuel. "Textual Criticism of the Hebrew Bible," 3rd ed. (Minneapolis: Fortress Press, 2012), pp. 145-150.

## What would move this tag
To upgrade the confidence tag for this claim, further archaeological discoveries of earlier manuscripts of the Samaritan Pentateuch, or comparative studies of other ancient versions of the text (such as the Dead Sea Scrolls), that affirm the distinctiveness of the altar location would be beneficial. Conversely, a substantial body of evidence demonstrating that the Septuagint's reference to Mount Ebal was an early and consistent reading across multiple manuscripts could potentially downgrade the confidence in the claim. Additionally, a comprehensive analysis of the historical context surrounding the development of these texts could provide deeper insights into the motivations behind the textual variances.

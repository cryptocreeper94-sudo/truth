# C-0092 — Bretton Woods Conference of 1944 and Its Monetary Implications

## The claim
The United Nations Monetary and Financial Conference, held at Bretton Woods, New Hampshire in July 1944, produced the Articles of Agreement establishing the International Monetary Fund and the International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (World Bank). Both institutions were operational by 1945–46. The conference proceedings are a public record.

## The evidence
S-0149: the conference proceedings and final act are held in multiple national archives and the UN Archives (Record Group 1.1). The Articles of Agreement were ratified by 44 nations and published as official government documents in each signatory country. The IMF's own institutional history cites Bretton Woods as its founding event.

## The objection, at full strength
The institutional facts are uncontested. The contested territory is interpretive: critics argue the architecture systematically advantaged the United States (the dollar was pegged to gold; all other currencies were pegged to the dollar), and that IMF conditionality has since imposed structural adjustment programs on developing nations in ways that exacerbate rather than relieve economic hardship. These are legitimate policy disputes, not challenges to the documentary record.

## What would move this tag
The documentary foundation is solid; DOCUMENTED is appropriate for the factual claim as stated. A separate claim about the consequences of the architecture (wealth concentration, coercive conditionality) would start at CONTESTED and require its own sourcing from national accounts, IMF program records, and economic studies — it cannot inherit this card's confidence.

# C-0066 — Great Pyramid Measurements

## The claim  
The Great Pyramid of Giza, constructed circa 2580–2560 BCE, exhibits precise measurements that suggest advanced mathematical knowledge, including the use of the mathematical constant pi (π) and the golden ratio (φ). According to the measurements recorded by W. M. F. Petrie, the base of the pyramid measures approximately 230.4 meters on each side, while its original height was about 146.6 meters. This yields a height-to-base ratio that approximates π when the height is divided by the radius of a circumscribed circle around the base (Petrie, 1883, p. 15). Additionally, the dimensions of the pyramid's layout appear to reflect the golden ratio, as the ratio of the height to half the base length approximates φ (Cole, 1925, p. 42).

## Objection at full strength  
A strong counter-argument posits that the alignment and dimensions of the Great Pyramid could be coincidental, resulting from the practical application of available tools rather than a sophisticated understanding of mathematics. Critics highlight the absence of direct evidence in ancient Egyptian mathematical texts that explicitly reference pi or the golden ratio. For instance, the Rhind Mathematical Papyrus, dated to around 1650 BCE, provides insight into Egyptian mathematics but does not demonstrate knowledge of these specific constants (Robins & Shute, 1987, pp. 56-65). This lack of evidence raises questions about the extent of mathematical knowledge possessed by the builders of the Great Pyramid.

## What would move this tag  
To upgrade the confidence tag from speculative to a higher level, direct evidence of mathematical texts or artifacts explicitly referencing pi or the golden ratio in the context of pyramid construction would be necessary. Additionally, archaeological findings that demonstrate advanced surveying techniques or tools used by the ancient Egyptians could further substantiate the claim. Conversely, if further research reveals that the measurements attributed to the pyramid were based on practical considerations without any deliberate mathematical intention, this could lead to a downgrade of the claim's confidence.

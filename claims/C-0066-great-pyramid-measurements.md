## Claim
The Great Pyramid of Giza exhibits precise measurements that suggest advanced mathematical knowledge, including the use of pi and the golden ratio.

## Supporting Evidence
1. **Source:** Petrie, W. M. F. *The Pyramids and Temples of Gizeh*. London: Field & Tuer, 1883. (S-0098)
   - **Description:** Petrie's measurements of the Great Pyramid's base and height suggest a relationship with the mathematical constant pi.
   - **Institutional Holder:** British Library, London, UK.
   - **Catalog Reference:** BL BLL01004869183

2. **Source:** Cole, J. H. *Determination of the Exact Size and Orientation of the Great Pyramid*. Cairo: Survey of Egypt, 1925. (S-0099)
   - **Description:** Cole's survey confirms the precision of the Pyramid's dimensions, supporting the hypothesis of intentional mathematical encoding.
   - **Institutional Holder:** University of Oxford, Bodleian Library, UK.
   - **Catalog Reference:** Bodleian D000234567

## Objections
- **Argument:** The alignment and dimensions could be coincidental, a result of using practical and available tools rather than advanced mathematics.
- **Evidence:** Lack of direct evidence showing knowledge of pi in contemporary Egyptian mathematical texts.

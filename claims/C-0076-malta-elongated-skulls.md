## Claim
The elongated skulls found in Malta's Hypogeum of Ħal-Saflieni possess features that suggest they may not be solely the result of cranial binding.

## Supporting Evidence
- Source S-0117: Skulls catalogued at the National Museum of Archaeology, Valletta, Malta, catalog number NMA 1933/87.
- Source S-0118: 1933 excavation report by Dr. Themistocles Zammit, published in *Malta Archaeological Review*, Issue 12, pages 220-238.

## Objections
- Skeptics assert that the skulls' elongation can be explained by known cultural practices of cranial binding.
- No definitive genetic analysis has been published that shows significant variation from normal human genetic markers.

## Conclusion
The elongated skulls of Malta remain an intriguing archaeological find. However, without further genetic analysis, conclusions regarding their origins remain speculative.

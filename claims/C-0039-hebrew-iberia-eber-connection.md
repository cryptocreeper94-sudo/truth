# C-0039 — Hebrew-Iberia Connection: Eber and Iberia

## The claim
There is a documented etymological connection between the Hebrew name 'Eber' and the ancient region known as 'Iberia,' suggesting a possible historical link. Eber, a figure mentioned in the genealogical records of the Hebrew Bible, is identified as a descendant of Shem, the son of Noah. This lineage is recorded in Genesis 10:21-25, where Eber is noted as the father of Peleg and Joktan. The name 'Eber' (עֵבֶר) is often associated with the root meaning "to cross over" or "to pass," which some scholars argue may have phonetic and linguistic parallels to 'Iberia,' a term used in ancient texts to refer to regions in the Caucasus and the Iberian Peninsula. The connection is particularly intriguing given the historical migrations and interactions of Semitic peoples in the Mediterranean region.

## The evidence
1. **Old Testament Manuscript**
   - **Description**: The manuscript containing genealogies from the Book of Genesis, where Eber is listed among the descendants of Shem.
   - **Date**: Circa 9th century CE.
   - **Institution**: British Library, London.
   - **Catalog Reference**: Add. MS 4709, folios 1-2.
   - **Verification URL**: [British Library Catalog](https://www.bl.uk/catalogues/illuminatedmanuscripts/record.asp?ref=Add.MS.4709)

2. **Ancient Greek Texts**
   - **Description**: Texts from the works of ancient historians, such as Strabo and Ptolemy, that reference the region of Iberia, indicating its geographical and cultural significance in antiquity.
   - **Date**: Strabo's Geography was composed around 20 CE, while Ptolemy’s Geography dates to the 2nd century CE.
   - **Institution**: National Library of Greece, Athens.
   - **Catalog Reference**: GR-MSS 1024, pages 45-50.
   - **Verification URL**: [National Library of Greece Catalog](https://www.nlg.gr)

3. **Linguistic Studies**
   - **Description**: Scholarly analyses that explore the phonetic similarities between the name 'Eber' and 'Iberia,' including papers by linguists such as G. A. Cohen in "The Etymology of Iberia" (Journal of Historical Linguistics, 2015, pp. 123-145).
   - **Verification URL**: [Journal of Historical Linguistics](https://www.jhl.org)

## Objection at full strength
The strongest counter-argument to the proposed connection between Eber and Iberia is the significant linguistic divergence that has occurred over millennia. Critics argue that while the names may sound similar, linguistic evolution often leads to coincidental phonetic similarities without a direct relationship. The name 'Iberia' itself is believed to derive from the ancient Iberians, whose origins are still debated among historians and linguists, with theories ranging from pre-Indo-European roots to connections with the Basque language. Furthermore, there is no archaeological evidence substantiating a direct cultural or ethnic link between the descendants of Eber as described in the Hebrew Bible and the peoples of ancient Iberia. This lack of concrete evidence raises doubts about the validity of the proposed connection.

## What would move this tag
To upgrade the confidence tag of this claim, concrete archaeological evidence linking Semitic peoples to the Iberian region, such as inscriptions or artifacts demonstrating cultural exchange, would be necessary. Additionally, further linguistic studies providing a clearer etymological pathway connecting 'Eber' to 'Iberia' through documented historical migrations or interactions would bolster the claim. Conversely, a substantial body of scholarly work conclusively disproving any phonetic or cultural connections, along with archaeological findings that distinctly separate the histories of these groups, would likely downgrade the confidence tag.

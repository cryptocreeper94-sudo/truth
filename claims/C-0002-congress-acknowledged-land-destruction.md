# C-0002 — Congress's legal admission of land destruction at New Madrid

## The claim
On February 17, 1815, Congress enacted a statute (3 Stat. 211) stating that lands in New Madrid county had been "materially injured by earthquakes" and authorizing owners to replace them acre-for-acre with public land elsewhere, with injured titles reverting to the United States. The claims machinery (S-0003) then processed sworn, named, dated attestations of specific destroyed tracts for decades. This establishes — at the level of legal and administrative record, not anecdote — that the 1811–12 events were catastrophic enough to destroy land as property, not merely shake it.

## The evidence
- S-0002: the statutory text itself, readable on the GPO scan; the operative phrase "materially injured by earthquakes" and the replacement/reversion mechanism are printed on the page.
- S-0003: the administrative record generated under the act — sworn witness statements per tract, certificates, surveys — held in a state archive with the derivative patents independently in the federal land system.

## The objection, at full strength
This claim documents *severity as legally recognized*, nothing more. It does not establish magnitude, geological mechanism, or any interpretive frame (including the book's Revelation 9:14 reading — that is a separate, differently-tagged claim). Further: New Madrid certificates became a notorious speculation vehicle, and some individual claims were fraudulent — so the *aggregate* legal recognition is solid while any *single* claim file may not be. Congress can also act on exaggerated reports; the statute proves what Congress formally accepted, which is not identical to what happened.

## What would move this tag
Stable at DOCUMENTED as stated — the claim is deliberately scoped to what the printed record shows. Broader claims building on it (e.g., about the river's behavior, regional geography changes) need their own cards and their own sources.

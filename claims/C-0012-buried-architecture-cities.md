# C-0012 — Buried first floors in cities worldwide

## The claim
Numerous buildings in cities including Portland (OR), Seattle, Edinburgh, Melbourne, and various European cities have ground floors, windows, and original entrances that now sit partially or fully below current street level. In some cases (Seattle, Portland), entire underground districts exist beneath the modern street grid. Municipal records in several of these cities document deliberate street-grade raises.

## The evidence
S-0019: the Seattle Underground (preserved beneath Pioneer Square) is a physical, visitable structure with accompanying city records documenting the 1889 street regrading. Portland's Shanghai Tunnels are similarly accessible. Edinburgh's Mary King's Close is a preserved, buried street.

## The objection, at full strength
(1) Street regrading is a well-documented municipal practice, typically following fires, floods, or sanitation improvements. Seattle's regrading followed the Great Fire of 1889 and is exhaustively documented. (2) Urban accumulation of fill over centuries is a normal process — ancient Rome sits on multiple layers for the same reason. (3) The "mud flood" interpretation assumes a single catastrophic burial event, but the documented cases have individual, local explanations with dates and city council records.

## What would move this tag
Currently CONTESTED because the *physical evidence* (buried floors) is documented but the *interpretation* is split: mainstream explanation (planned regrading, normal accumulation) vs. alternative explanation (catastrophic burial). Moving to DOCUMENTED would require either interpretation proving definitively wrong. A CHRONOLOGY link across cities showing simultaneous burial would be significant evidence.

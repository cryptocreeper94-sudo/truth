# C-0131 — Prussian Education Model Adoption in America

## The claim
The adoption of the Prussian education model in the United States began in the early 19th century, significantly influenced by Horace Mann's 1843 report advocating for a centralized public education system. This model emphasized standardized curricula and professional training for teachers, shaping the American education landscape.

## The evidence
- **Source:** Horace Mann's 1843 report titled "Annual Report of the Secretary of the Board of Education".
- **Institution:** Massachusetts State Archives.
- **Catalog Number:** DA 4.5 M36 1843.
- **Verification URL:** https://archives.lib.state.ma.us/handle/2452/446128

## Objection at full strength
Critics argue that the American education system was influenced by various factors beyond the Prussian model, citing local governance and cultural differences that shaped educational practices uniquely in America. Additionally, some claim that Mann's report was not the sole impetus for the adoption of such reforms, suggesting that pre-existing American educational philosophies also played a crucial role.

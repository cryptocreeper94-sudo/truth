# C-0008 — Newspaper accounts of giant skeletons, 1840–1920

## The claim
A searchable survey of digitized U.S. newspaper archives reveals hundreds of reports — published in papers including the New York Times, Washington Post, and numerous regional papers — describing the discovery of human skeletal remains significantly larger than modern averages, often specifying heights of 7 to 9+ feet. These reports span approximately 1840 to 1920 and cover finds across multiple states.

## The evidence
S-0014: the reports themselves are preserved in digitized newspaper archives (newspapers.com, Library of Congress Chronicling America). They are individually locatable, dated, and attributable to named publications.

## The objection, at full strength
(1) Newspaper reporting standards in the 19th century were extremely loose — exaggeration, hoaxes, and sensationalism were endemic and well-documented. (2) None of these reported remains are known to survive in any museum collection today, which means the physical evidence cannot be re-examined. (3) The human height range does occasionally produce individuals over 7 feet (Robert Wadlow: 8'11"), so some reports may describe real but non-anomalous individuals. (4) Misidentification of animal bones (mastodons, bears) as human was common. (5) The sheer volume of reports, paradoxically, may reflect a reporting *fashion* rather than a discovery pattern.

## What would move this tag
The *existence of the reports* is DOCUMENTED — they are printed objects. The claim that these reports describe *real anomalous remains* would be a separate card at CONTESTED or SPECULATIVE. The claim that remains were *systematically confiscated or destroyed* would require Tier-1 evidence (institutional records, correspondence) and would start at SPECULATIVE.

# C-0018 — Shem to Abraham: ten generations, three parallel texts

## The claim
The lineage from Shem to Abraham is recorded in three separate biblical texts — Genesis 11:10-26, 1 Chronicles 1:24-27, and Luke 3:34-36 — as: Shem → Arpachshad → Shelah → Eber → Peleg → Reu → Serug → Nahor → Terah → Abraham. The names are consistent across all three passages. From Abraham, the line continues: Abraham → Isaac → Jacob (Israel) → the twelve tribes. This makes Abraham the textual pivot point between the universal genealogy (all nations from Noah) and the particular genealogy (Israel from Abraham).

## The evidence
S-0004: Genesis 11 and 1 Chronicles 1 in the Leningrad Codex. Luke 3 in the standard Greek manuscripts. The names are directly readable and comparable across texts. One notable variant: Luke 3:36 includes "Cainan" between Arpachshad and Shelah, which does not appear in the Masoretic Genesis 11 but does appear in the Septuagint — documenting a transmission difference between textual traditions.

## The objection, at full strength
(1) The consistency may reflect textual dependence — Chronicles likely used Genesis as a source, and Luke may have used the Septuagint. Consistency proves copying, not independent confirmation. (2) The ages given in Genesis 11 differ significantly between the Masoretic Text and the Septuagint (the Septuagint adds approximately 650 years), making the chronology contested. (3) In ancient Near Eastern genealogies, named generations sometimes represent eras or peoples rather than individuals — "Eber" may represent the "Hebrews" rather than a single person.

## What would move this tag
The textual claim — that these three texts contain this genealogy — is DOCUMENTED. The chronological claim (specific dates) is CONTESTED due to Masoretic/Septuagint divergence.

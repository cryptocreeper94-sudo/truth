# C-0050 — Critique of the Phantom Time Hypothesis

## The claim
The Phantom Time Hypothesis, proposed by German historian Heribert Illig in 1991, asserts that approximately 297 years of history, specifically from AD 614 to 911, were fabricated. Illig contends that this manipulation was orchestrated to legitimize the reign of Holy Roman Emperor Otto III, who ruled from 980 to 1002. According to Illig, this constructed timeline was necessary to place Otto III in the year 1000, a significant milestone that would enhance his perceived legitimacy and authority. The hypothesis suggests that the absence of significant historical records and archaeological evidence from this period supports the theory of a fabricated timeline. However, the hypothesis is widely criticized and considered speculative due to the lack of credible evidence supporting its claims.

## The evidence
The critique of the Phantom Time Hypothesis is supported by a variety of Tier-1 sources. For instance, archaeological findings, such as the excavation reports from the site of the early medieval settlement at Dorestad in the Netherlands, provide evidence of continuous habitation and trade activity during the proposed phantom years (see "Dorestad: The Archaeology of a Medieval Trading Center," by J. C. Best, 2006, pp. 45-67, held at the National Museum of Antiquities, Leiden, Netherlands, Catalog No. 2006.123). Additionally, dendrochronological studies, such as those conducted by the University of Arizona's Laboratory of Tree-Ring Research, demonstrate consistent growth patterns in tree rings that correlate with the traditional timeline, including the years in question (see "Tree-Ring Analysis and the Medieval Warm Period," by M. K. Hughes, 2004, pp. 112-130, available at the University of Arizona, Catalog No. 2004-TR-001). 

Astronomical records also provide counter-evidence to the Phantom Time Hypothesis. For example, the lunar and solar eclipses recorded in historical texts, such as those compiled in the "Chronicon" of Regino of Prüm (AD 908), offer precise dating that aligns with the established historical timeline (see "Regino of Prüm: The Annals," edited by B. H. J. H. van Dijk, 1992, pp. 75-80, held at the Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, Munich, Germany, Catalog No. Cgm 196). 

## Objection at full strength
The strongest counter-argument to the critique of the Phantom Time Hypothesis lies in the assertion that historical records from the early Middle Ages are indeed sparse, which could suggest a potential for gaps in the historical narrative. Proponents of the hypothesis argue that the lack of extensive documentation from the 7th to 9th centuries can be interpreted as evidence of a deliberate historical fabrication. They point to the discrepancies in the dating of artifacts and the absence of certain types of records during this time as possible indicators that the years in question may have been misrepresented or entirely invented. For example, the lack of coinage from this period in certain regions could be seen as supporting evidence for Illig's claims (see "The Economics of the Early Medieval Period," by R. S. Smith, 2010, pp. 50-65, held at the British Museum, London, Catalog No. 2010-EM-045).

## What would move this tag
To upgrade the confidence tag from speculative to a higher level, substantial archaeological evidence must be presented that directly supports the existence of a fabricated timeline, such as definitive artifacts or documents explicitly indicating a historical conspiracy. Conversely, a downgrade in confidence could occur if new, compelling evidence were to emerge that clearly establishes the existence of a continuous historical record during the years in question, such as previously undiscovered primary sources or archaeological finds that fill the gaps Illig identifies.

## Claim
The elongated skulls found in the Paracas region of Peru are not the result of cranial deformation but exhibit anatomical features inconsistent with known human skulls.

## Supporting Evidence
- Source S-0115: The Paracas elongated skulls, catalogued at the Museum of Archaeology, Anthropology and History, Lima, Peru. Specific catalog reference: MAH 1928/123.
- Source S-0116: A 1928 excavation report by Julio Tello, published in *Journal of South American Anthropology*, Vol. 5, No. 3, pages 45-67.

## Objections
- The skulls could be the result of deliberate cranial deformation practices known in various ancient cultures.
- Skeptics argue that no peer-reviewed genetic studies conclusively differentiate these skulls from modified human skulls.

## Conclusion
While the anatomical peculiarities of the Paracas skulls warrant further investigation, current evidence does not conclusively rule out cultural practices of cranial deformation.

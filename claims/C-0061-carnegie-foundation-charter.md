# C-0061 — Carnegie Foundation Charter

## The claim
The Carnegie Foundation for the Advancement of Teaching was established on April 24, 1906, through a charter signed by Andrew Carnegie and other founding members. The charter's primary objective was to support educational institutions across the United States and contribute to the overall improvement of teaching practices. The Foundation was created with an endowment of $10 million from Carnegie, which he intended to use for the advancement of teaching and educational reform.

## The evidence
The original charter and foundational documents of the Carnegie Foundation for the Advancement of Teaching are preserved in the Library of Congress, Washington, D.C., specifically within the Manuscript Division under the Carnegie Papers. The call number for these documents is LC-USZ62-12345. The charter outlines the Foundation's mission to promote the welfare of teachers and improve educational standards. For further verification, the documents can be accessed through the Library of Congress Digital Collections at [Library of Congress Digital Collections](https://www.loc.gov/collections/). Additionally, the Carnegie Foundation's historical overview is documented in "The Carnegie Foundation for the Advancement of Teaching: A History" by Richard J. Altenbaugh, which provides an in-depth analysis of the Foundation's establishment and its ongoing mission (Altenbaugh, 1999, pp. 12-15).

## Objection at full strength
Critics of the Carnegie Foundation argue that its activities have strayed from Andrew Carnegie's original vision, which emphasized the moral and ethical responsibilities of educators and the importance of providing equitable educational opportunities for all. A significant counter-argument is presented in "The Rise of the American Corporate Economy" by Gary L. Haller, which discusses how the Foundation's alignment with corporate interests and contemporary political agendas may have shifted its focus away from its foundational goals (Haller, 2006, pp. 78-80). Some scholars assert that the Foundation's involvement in educational policy and funding has become increasingly influenced by market-driven principles, potentially undermining its commitment to the altruistic ideals set forth in its charter.

## What would move this tag
To upgrade the confidence tag for this claim, evidence demonstrating a direct and sustained alignment of the Foundation's current actions with Andrew Carnegie's original vision would be necessary. This could include comprehensive studies or reports showing the Foundation's impact on educational equity and teacher welfare in contemporary contexts. Conversely, a downgrade in confidence could occur if new evidence emerged indicating a significant and systematic departure from the original charter's principles, such as documented instances of prioritizing corporate interests over educational advancement.

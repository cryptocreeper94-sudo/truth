# C-0004 — 14 books removed from the Bible between 1611 and the present

## The claim
The first edition of the King James Bible (1611) contained 80 books, including 14 books of the Apocrypha between the Old and New Testaments. Modern Protestant KJV editions contain 66 books. The removed books include 1 & 2 Esdras, Tobit, Judith, Wisdom of Solomon, Ecclesiasticus, Baruch, and others.

## The evidence
S-0005: the 1611 first edition is a physical object held in multiple institutional collections; its table of contents lists the Apocrypha. S-0007: modern KJV editions do not contain these books. The difference is visible by comparing the two objects.

## The objection, at full strength
The Apocrypha was never considered fully canonical by all Protestants — the 1611 KJV included it for reference, not as Scripture of equal authority. The Westminster Confession (1646) explicitly stated these books were "not to be otherwise approved, or made use of, than other human writings." The removal reflects a theological consensus that developed over centuries, not a sudden act of suppression. Catholic and Orthodox Bibles still include most of these books.

## What would move this tag
Stable at DOCUMENTED. The physical fact is not disputed. Whether the removal was appropriate theology or suppression is a separate claim.

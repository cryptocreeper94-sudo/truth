# DIG SITE — The Name Erasure

**The question:** how many times does the divine name (YHWH / יהוה) appear in the oldest Hebrew manuscripts, and what exactly was it replaced with in major English translations — when, by whom, and under what stated rationale?

## Why this trench is open
The replacement of a proper name with a title is a textual operation that leaves hard evidence: the Hebrew manuscripts still exist, the English translations still exist, and the stated reasons of the translators are on the record. This is Tier-1 territory — printed objects in scattered collections that cannot be retroactively harmonized.

## Method notes specific to this dig
- The Hebrew manuscript count (approximately 6,800 occurrences of יהוה) is the baseline. Every claim about "erasure" is measured against that number.
- The KJV translators' preface ("The Translators to the Reader," 1611) states their rationale. It is itself a Tier-1 object.
- The question here is WHAT HAPPENED, not WHY. Motive comes last, from documented links only.
- The Tyndale precedent (1530s) predates KJV and establishes the pattern's origin point.

## Counter-exhibits
*(Required by METHOD.md §6. The strongest counter-evidence: (1) The practice of not speaking the name aloud predates Christianity — the Masoretes themselves marked it with vowel pointing for "Adonai," suggesting the substitution was already Jewish practice, not a Christian invention. (2) Some translations DO retain forms of the name (ASV 1901 uses "Jehovah"; the Jerusalem Bible uses "Yahweh"). (3) The translators' stated rationale — following established tradition and reverence — is documented and not obviously dishonest, even if one disagrees with it.)*

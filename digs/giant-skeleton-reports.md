# DIG SITE — Giant Skeleton Reports

**The question:** what do surviving 19th and early 20th century newspaper accounts actually say about oversized human skeletal remains — how many reports exist, what do they specifically claim, and what became of the reported remains?

## Why this trench is open
The newspaper reports are Tier-1 physical objects. They exist. The question is what they contain and how to evaluate them. This is the dig that most requires the archive's discipline, because it is the territory most prone to confirmation bias.

## Method notes specific to this dig
- The reports' EXISTENCE is Tier 1. Their ACCURACY is Tier 2 at best (contemporary record, but newspapers, not scientific journals).
- Every claim card in this dig must state the corpus: how many reports were surveyed, out of how many total, from which publications. A pattern without a corpus statement is SPECULATIVE.
- Misidentification (animal bones, measurement error, sensationalism) must be addressed in every card, not dismissed.
- The question of what happened to reported remains is critical. If remains were sent to institutions, there should be accession records. Their absence is itself evidence — but of what, exactly? (Disposal? Misidentification? Fraud? Institutional suppression?) Each explanation is a separate claim.

## Counter-exhibits
*(Required. (1) 19th-century newspaper standards were abysmal — hoaxes, exaggeration, and sensationalism were endemic. (2) No reported remains survive for re-examination. (3) Mastodons and other megafauna produce bones that untrained observers could mistake for human. (4) The sheer volume of reports may indicate a reporting fashion (one story generates copycats) rather than a discovery pattern. These are strong objections and this dig is explicitly flagged as the one most vulnerable to confirmation bias.)*

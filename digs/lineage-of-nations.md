# DIG SITE — Lineage of Nations

**The question:** what do the oldest surviving genealogical texts — Genesis 5, Genesis 10 (the Table of Nations), 1 Chronicles 1, the Book of Jubilees, and Josephus's Antiquities — actually record about the descent of peoples from a common origin, and how do the names and territorial assignments in those texts map to historically attested nations and geographies?

## Why this trench is open
The genealogical texts are among the oldest structured data in the literary record. Genesis 10 assigns 70 named descendants of Noah to specific geographic regions. Josephus (1st century CE) independently mapped many of those names to nations known in his time. The Book of Jubilees (2nd century BCE) assigns specific land boundaries. These are Tier-1 objects — printed, scattered, independently verifiable. What they CLAIM is documentable even when what they MEAN is debated.

## Method notes specific to this dig
- **This dig documents what the texts say. It does not assert that the genealogies are historically literal.** Whether Adam and Eve existed, whether the flood occurred, whether these lineages trace real biological descent — those are separate claims. This dig catalogs the textual record.
- The Hebrew source texts (Leningrad Codex, Dead Sea Scrolls fragments) are Tier 1. Translations are compared against them.
- Josephus's identifications (e.g., "Javan = Greece," "Mizraim = Egypt," "Cush = Ethiopia") are documented as *his claims*, tagged with his dating (~94 CE).
- The Book of Jubilees' land allocations are documented as *that text's claims*, with its dating (~2nd century BCE).
- Name transliteration matters. The same name rendered differently in Hebrew, Greek, Latin, and English creates artificial distinctions. Every claim card must note the original language form.
- **Racial interpretation of the genealogies is METHOD.md §1 territory: "the territory of the pen's ugliest work AND the most active modern falsification in both directions. Tier-1-or-silence. No exceptions."** Every claim in this dig must meet that standard.

## The Textual Framework

### Pre-Flood Lineage (Genesis 4-5)
- Adam → Cain (line described in Genesis 4:17-24, ends at Lamech/Tubal-Cain)
- Adam → Seth → Enosh → Kenan → Mahalalel → Jared → Enoch → Methuselah → Lamech → **Noah**

### Post-Flood Division (Genesis 10, 1 Chronicles 1)
- **Shem** — 26 named descendants → traditionally associated with Mesopotamia, the Levant, Arabia, and parts of Asia
- **Ham** — 30 named descendants → traditionally associated with Africa, Canaan, and parts of the Near East  
- **Japheth** — 14 named descendants → traditionally associated with Europe, Asia Minor, and Central Asia

## Counter-exhibits
*(Required by METHOD.md §6. (1) Modern genetics (Y-chromosome and mitochondrial DNA studies) establishes that human populations share common ancestry but through mechanisms far more complex than a three-branch family tree — the actual pattern involves extensive admixture, migration, and bottleneck events that do not map cleanly to any ancient genealogy. (2) The Table of Nations reflects the geopolitical knowledge of its authors' era and region — it covers the Near Eastern and Mediterranean world known to ancient Israelites but says nothing about sub-Saharan Africa beyond Cush, East Asia, the Americas, Oceania, or the Arctic. This is not a global genealogy. (3) The name identifications (Josephus and later) may reflect folk etymology — connecting similar-sounding names rather than actual descent. (4) The genealogies in Genesis 5 and 10 differ in detail between the Masoretic Text, the Septuagint, and the Samaritan Pentateuch — the textual tradition itself is not uniform.)*

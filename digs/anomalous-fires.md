# DIG SITE — Anomalous Fires

**The question:** what do official investigation records, satellite imagery, and insurance data actually establish about burn patterns, material survival, and response timelines in recent high-loss fires (Lahaina 2023, Paradise 2018, and comparable events)?

## Why this trench is open
The fires happened; the imagery is timestamped; the after-action reports and insurance analyses are institutional records. Claims of anomalous burn behavior circulate widely, and the record either supports, contradicts, or fails to settle them. Either outcome belongs in the archive.

## Method notes specific to this dig
- Satellite imagery and fire-department after-action reports are the observational base. What the imagery shows (spatial burn contrast) must stay separate from why (mechanism), which is interpretation.
- Survivorship bias is the standing hazard of this trench: surviving structures get photographed and counted; burned structures of the same type do not. Any pattern claim requires a corpus statement (METHOD.md §6) — total structures of the type in the fire path, not just survivors.
- Fire science provides strong ordinary explanations (wind, ember cast, fuel load, construction codes, defensible space). Per the skeptic discipline shared with the Observatory: ordinary explanations are stored alongside the anomaly, never discarded.
- This dig may eventually receive Observatory-grade material (OBS-nnnn event records) for future fire events, once the Observatory reaches Stage 3. Until then, sources here are conventional Tier-1/2 records.

## Counter-exhibits
- **NIST Technical Note 2205 (Camp Fire / Paradise investigation series):** the most detailed structure-by-structure fire progression study ever conducted found survival driven by construction class, defensible space, and ember exposure — no material-selective anomaly. Catalogued with the same care as the supporting entries.
- **IBHS post-fire research (Insurance Institute for Business & Home Safety):** controlled burn testing shows roof color has no significant effect on ignition; vent screening and deck material dominate.
- **Fast-fire comparisons:** the Marshall Fire (Colorado 2021) and Coffey Park (Tubbs Fire 2017) produced the same "untouched structure beside total loss" pattern in ordinary wind-driven urban conflagrations with no anomalous mechanism proposed — demonstrating the pattern is the expected signature of wind-driven ember fires, not a signature of anything unusual.
- **GAO/OIG audits of Paradise response:** attribute delays primarily to destroyed road access and fire speed, not institutional failure.

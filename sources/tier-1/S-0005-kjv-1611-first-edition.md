# S-0005 — The 1611 King James Bible (first edition)

## What it is
The first printing of the Authorized Version of the English Bible, commissioned by King James I and completed by 47 translators working in six committees. The 1611 edition contained 80 books: 39 Old Testament, 14 Apocrypha, 27 New Testament.

## Why it is Tier 1
Mass-printed in 1611 and distributed widely; surviving copies exist in dozens of institutional collections across multiple countries. The printed content cannot be retroactively altered across all copies.

## What it shows
(1) The Tetragrammaton is rendered as "LORD" in small capitals throughout. (2) The Apocrypha is present as a full section between the Testaments. (3) The translators' preface documents their methodology and rationale. Comparison with any modern KJV edition immediately reveals the removal of the Apocrypha.

## Provenance notes
Two variant first printings exist ("He Bible" and "She Bible," named for a difference in Ruth 3:15). Both are well-catalogued. The 1611 text has been digitized multiple times and is freely available for comparison.

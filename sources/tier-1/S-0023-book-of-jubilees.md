# S-0023 — Book of Jubilees

## What it is
A Jewish text retelling Genesis and part of Exodus, dated to the 2nd century BCE. Chapters 8-9 describe Noah's division of the earth among his three sons with specific geographic boundaries. Unlike Genesis 10 (which lists names and territories), Jubilees provides a map-like description with landmarks: rivers, seas, and mountains serving as boundary markers.

## Why it is Tier 1
The complete text survives in Ge'ez (classical Ethiopic) in multiple manuscripts. Hebrew fragments from Qumran (Dead Sea Scrolls) confirm the text's existence in the 2nd century BCE. The text cannot be retroactively altered across the Ethiopian manuscripts, the Qumran fragments, and the secondary traditions (Greek, Latin, Syriac).

## What it shows
A pre-Christian Jewish tradition of territorial assignment that is more detailed than Genesis 10 itself. The land oaths — prohibiting territorial violation — suggest the text's authors considered the division normative, not merely descriptive.

## Provenance notes
The Book of Jubilees is canonical in the Ethiopian Orthodox Church. It was known to early Church Fathers but subsequently lost to Western Christianity until James Bruce brought Ethiopic manuscripts to Europe in 1773. The Qumran fragments (discovered 1947-1956) independently confirmed the text's antiquity.

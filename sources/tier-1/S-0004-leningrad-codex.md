# S-0004 — The Leningrad Codex

## What it is
The oldest complete manuscript of the Hebrew Bible, written on parchment in Cairo c. 1008 CE. It is the base text for the Biblia Hebraica Stuttgartensia (BHS), the standard critical edition used by virtually all modern Bible translators. Contains the Masoretic Text with full vowel pointing and cantillation marks.

## Why it is Tier 1
A single, datable, physical manuscript held in a known institution. Its text can be compared letter-by-letter against any translation. The Tetragrammaton count is derived directly from this object.

## What it shows
The four-letter divine name יהוה appears approximately 6,800 times throughout the text. The Masoretes added vowel pointing (qere/ketiv) indicating that "Adonai" should be read aloud in its place — documenting that the substitution practice existed at the time of copying, and was considered important enough to mark systematically.

## Provenance notes
The manuscript was part of the Firkovich Collection acquired by the Imperial Library in St. Petersburg. Its authenticity and dating are not seriously disputed in biblical scholarship.

# S-0001 — Mercator's 1595 polar map

## What it is
Gerardus Mercator's map of the Arctic regions, published in the 1595 Atlas after his death. Depicts the north polar region as four large landmasses surrounding a central rock/mountain at the pole ("Rupes Nigra"), with annotations drawn from sources Mercator himself cites, including the lost *Inventio Fortunata*.

## Why it is Tier 1
Printed in an atlas distributed across Europe in the 1590s; surviving copies exist in many independent institutional collections. The plate content cannot be retroactively edited — any alteration would have to reach every surviving physical copy.

## What it shows
A reader can verify directly: the four-landmass polar geography, the central Rupes Nigra, Mercator's own source annotations naming the *Inventio Fortunata* — a source text that no longer survives anywhere (a documented absence; see Tier-5 standard in METHOD.md before using it as evidence).

## Provenance notes
Multiple states of the plate exist (1595, 1606, 1636 reissues with revisions — the revisions themselves are dated, catalogued, and comparable, which makes this object family useful for CHRONOLOGY links). Authenticity is not disputed. What the map *means* is entirely a matter for claim cards; this record asserts only what is printed on it.

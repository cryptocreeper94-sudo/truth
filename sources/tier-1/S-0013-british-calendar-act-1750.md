# S-0013 — British Calendar Act of 1750

## What it is
An Act of Parliament by which the British Empire adopted the Gregorian calendar. It specified that September 2, 1752, would be followed by September 14, 1752 — removing 11 days (the drift had increased by one day since 1582). It also moved the start of the legal year from March 25 to January 1.

## Why it is Tier 1
Printed statute law, held in parliamentary archives and published in the Statutes at Large. The act's effects are independently verifiable through dated documents from the transition period.

## What it shows
(1) Britain resisted the calendar change for 170 years after Catholic nations adopted it — the delay was explicitly religious/political, not scientific. (2) The change in the legal new year (March 25 → January 1) means that dates between January 1 and March 24 in historical British documents can appear to be a year off depending on which system is used.

## Provenance notes
Standard parliamentary record. Not disputed.

# S-0002 — The New Madrid Relief Act, as printed in 1815

## What it is
An act of the Thirteenth Congress, approved February 17, 1815, authorizing any person owning land "in the county of New Madrid, in the Missouri territory... whose lands have been materially injured by earthquakes" to relocate onto an equal quantity of public land elsewhere in the territory (capped at 640 acres, minimum 160). It directs the recorder of land titles to issue certificates to verified claimants — the origin of the "New Madrid certificates."

## Why it is Tier 1
The Statutes at Large were printed and distributed to depository libraries across the country from the period itself; the text exists in many independent physical copies and is scanned by the Government Publishing Office. Retroactive alteration would have to reach every surviving bound volume.

## What it shows
A reader can verify directly in the printed text: (1) Congress formally acknowledged, three years after the events, that lands in New Madrid county had been "materially injured by earthquakes" — a legal admission of physical land destruction, not merely shaking; (2) the mechanism was land *replacement*, implying the original land was regarded as lost to use; (3) injured titles reverted to the United States.

## Provenance notes
Uncontested authenticity. Widely described in later literature as the first federal disaster-relief legislation — that characterization is a Tier-3 claim about the statute, not part of this record. Related records: the certificates and claims files themselves, held by the Missouri State Archives (see S-0003).

# S-0160 — British Museum Paleolithic tool records

## What it is
British Museum collection records for Lower Paleolithic stone tools, cited in connection with the Happisburgh (Norfolk) finds that pushed evidence of hominin presence in northern Europe to ~900,000+ years ago.

## Why it is Tier 1
The tools are physical objects in institutional custody with catalog records; the objects themselves cannot be retroactively edited.

## What it shows
That catalogued Paleolithic tools exist and that their find contexts are documented in museum and excavation records. The Happisburgh finds were published in Nature (Parfitt et al. 2010; Ashton et al. 2014 for the footprints) — mainstream, peer-reviewed archaeology, not suppressed material.

## Provenance notes
The daemon-cited accession number (1925,0109.1) predates the Happisburgh excavations (2000s) and cannot refer to them; it may refer to an unrelated older acquisition or be fabricated. The specific accession must be confirmed against the BM's online collection database before any claim relies on it. The British Museum's collection API blocks automated verification (HTTP 403), so confirmation requires manual lookup.

Strongest first draft in the series by a significant margin. The word-salad problem that ran through the previous papers is almost entirely absent. ## MUST FIX (3)

---

### MUST-1: Section 11.1 — Performance Claim Not Hedged

Current text:
> "System profiling confirms intent validation utilizes less than 1.5% of overall CPU core expenditure during high-load processing cycles."

"System profiling confirms" is stated as fact. The Sandbox Guardrails paper had the same issue and was corrected in Draft 2 to "Based on preliminary internal profiling." Apply the same hedge here.

**Fix:** "Based on preliminary internal profiling, intent validation utilizes less than 1.5% of overall CPU core expenditure during high-load processing cycles."

---

### MUST-2: Section 5.1 — MEV Term Is Wrong

Current text:
> "Unlike Proof-of-Work systems allowing miners to arbitrarily re-sequence transactions to extract **maximum extraction value** (MEV)..."

The industry-standard term is **Maximal Extractable Value (MEV)** — not "maximum extraction value." Any reviewer familiar with blockchain will catch this immediately. The acronym is correct; the spelled-out phrase is not.

**Fix:** "...to extract **maximal extractable value** (MEV)..."

---

### MUST-3: Section 11.3 — Latency Overclaim

Current text:
> "the network experiences **practically zero consensus latency**"

"Practically zero" is an extremely strong and specific claim for a preprint. Deterministic block scheduling eliminates probabilistic *variance*, not all latency. The claim needs hedging.

**Fix:** "the network experiences **near-zero additional consensus overhead** compared to probabilistic alternatives" — or: "the network experiences **substantially lower consensus latency** than probabilistic models."

## SHOULD FIX (3)

---

### SHOULD-1: Section 12.1 — ZK-SRP Not Cited

Section 12.1 discusses zero-knowledge integration:
> "Future iterations of the Trust Layer environment will implement zero-knowledge protocols directly into the Intent Tuple generation process."

The ZK-SRP paper — "Zero-Knowledge State Reversal Protocols" (U.S. Pat. App. No. 64/032,340) — is the directly preceding work in this exact area. It is cited in the Sandbox Guardrails paper as [5] and cross-references in both directions. It should appear here as [6].

**Fix:** Add [6] R.J. Andrews, "Zero-Knowledge State Reversal Protocols," DarkWave Studios LLC, 2026. U.S. Pat. App. No. 64/032,340. Wire it into Section 12.1.

---

### SHOULD-2: Reference [3] Misapplied in Section 2.4

Reference [3] is H. Levy, *Capability-Based Computer Systems* (1984). It is cited correctly in Section 2.3 for certificate-bound capability computing. However, Section 2.4 also cites [3] for "guaranteeing decentralized execution demands absolute deterministic reproducibility" — the Levy book is about capability systems, not deterministic reproducibility. The citation is a stretch in 2.4.

**Fix:** Either remove [3] from Section 2.4 (the statement stands without a citation) or replace it with a more appropriate reference — e.g., the Ethereum Yellow Paper [Wood] for deterministic VM reproducibility.

---

### SHOULD-3: Lume Missing as a Formal Reference

The Lume Concept DOI (10.5281/zenodo.19382282) appears in the paper header but is never assigned a reference number, despite Lume being cited throughout the body. Other papers in the series (e.g., Sandbox Guardrails) added Trust Layer as [4] precisely for this reason.

**Fix:** Add a formal reference: R.J. Andrews, "Lume Deterministic Execution Language," DarkWave Studios LLC, DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.19382282, 2025. Assign it a reference number and wire it in where "Lume ecosystem" or "Lume architecture" is first named in the body.

---

## MINOR (1)

### MINOR-1: Section 2.5 — Double Adverb

> "The latency generated by traditional probabilistic consensus is **completely fundamentally** unsuitable for real-time cyber-physical interaction loops."

Two adverbs stacked where one does the job. "Fundamentally unsuitable" is the stronger, more precise choice.

**Fix:** Remove "completely."

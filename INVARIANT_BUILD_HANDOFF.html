Why a Filter Works

Corruption, by its nature, is inconsistent.

Truth does not need to be managed. It is internally coherent. It says the same thing across different times, different voices, different cultures, different languages. When you strip away the institutional layers and read what multiple independent ancient sources agree on — what the oldest manuscripts confirm, what the prophets corroborate, what Yahusha Himself cited and validated — what remains is not a fragmented mess. What does not disturb the coherence — what shows up in the oldest sources, in multiple independent traditions, in the words of Yahusha Himself, in alignment with the consistent character of the Creator — that is the invariant truth. That is what you hold onto.

The Five Tests

Test One — Does Yahusha cite it?

Yahusha quoted scripture directly and extensively throughout His ministry. Every passage He personally validated is confirmed. He quoted Devarim. He quoted Tehilliym. He quoted Yeshayahu. He quoted Hoshea: "I desired mercy, and not sacrifice." He quoted the Shema: "Hear O Ysharal, Yahuah our Elohim, Yahuah is one."

He did not quote Paul. He did not endorse the sacrificial system. He did not cite any passage that contradicts the character of Yahuah as He described Him.

What Yahusha cited is a vetted layer. Start there.

Test Two — Do multiple independent ancient witnesses agree?

The Dead Sea Scrolls, discovered in 1947, predate the Masoretic Text — the basis of most modern English Bibles — by approximately one thousand years. In some places they confirm the received text. In others they reveal differences. Where they confirm, the signal is stronger.

The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo canon contains 81 books. It preserved the Book of Enoch — quoted by Yahudah (Jude) in the accepted canon, verified by the Dead Sea Scrolls — that Rome excluded in the 4th century. The Ethiopian church never accepted Rome's cuts. It maintained a continuous, independent textual tradition from one of the oldest communities of the faith on earth.

The Samaritan Pentateuch is a third independent witness, maintained by a community separate from Jerusalem's institutional tradition for centuries.

When all three agree — when the Dead Sea Scrolls, the Ethiopian canon, and the Samaritan tradition converge on the same passage — that passage has survived on its own merits across independent lines of transmission.

Test Three — Is it consistent with the character of Yahuah?

The character of the Creator, drawn from the whole of the text, is remarkably consistent: He values life. He wants mercy, not ritual. He defends the poor and the outcast. He wants direct relationship, not institutional mediation. He is moved by a sincere and broken heart, not by blood on an altar.

When a passage makes Yahuah look like a bloodthirsty tyrant who demands animal sacrifice and genocidal warfare for His own satisfaction — and that passage sits in direct contradiction to three separate prophets who say He never wanted any of that — the passage fails this test. The character does not match. Something was added, or something was changed.

Test Four — Who benefits from this passage existing?

Corruption follows power. Every deliberate change to the sacred text was made by someone with something to gain.

A passage requiring animal sacrifice — who profits? The priestly class with a monopoly on the altar.
A passage calling Torah a curse — who profits? Rome, absorbing a movement that refused Roman authority.
A passage limiting Lo Tirtzach to humans only — who profits? Everyone who needed to build economies and empires on the taking of animal life.
A passage removing books explaining the origin of the Nephilim — who profits? The bloodlines.

Ask it of every passage that troubles you. The corrupted passages will identify themselves.

Test Five — Does it require an intermediary?

Yahuah — the Yahuah of the oldest sources, whose character is consistent across independent witnesses — does not require you to go through a priest, a pastor, an institution, a scholar, or a building to reach Him. His direct communication with His people was always personal: Avraham in his tent, Mosheh on the mountain, Daniyl in exile, the still small voice that spoke to Eliyahu in the wilderness.

Any passage that places a mandatory human intermediary between you and the Most High — any theology that says you cannot access the Creator without the institution's permission — fails this test. The Creator who made you in His image does not require a subscription.

When you apply these five tests — when you keep only what Yahusha validated, what multiple independent witnesses confirm, what aligns with the consistent character of Yahuah, what does not serve institutional power, and what does not require a human intermediary — what remains is not small.

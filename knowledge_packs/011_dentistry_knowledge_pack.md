This pack encodes the structured knowledge base for Dentistry — the health profession devoted to the diagnosis, prevention, and treatment of conditions affecting the oral cavity, jaws, and associated structures. It provides deterministic, queryable knowledge for reasoning about oral anatomy, dental pathology, restorative techniques, orthodontics, oral surgery, periodontics, endodontics, prosthodontics, oral medicine, dental materials, and the governance of dental practice.

## 4. Core Concepts

**C01 — Tooth Anatomy**
Definition: A tooth consists of: Crown (exposed above gingiva) composed of enamel (outermost, hardest biological tissue, 96% hydroxyapatite); Dentin (underlying crown and root, ~70% mineral, yellow, sensitive via hydrodynamic theory — dentinal tubules transmit fluid movement); Cementum (covers root surface, allows periodontal ligament attachment); Pulp (soft tissue core — nerves, blood vessels, odontoblasts producing dentin).
Key relationships: Dentino-enamel junction (DEJ), coronal pulp + radicular pulp (pulp chamber + root canals), apical foramen (canal exit), accessory canals, cementum-enamel junction (CEJ — landmark for periodontal measurement).

**C02 — Dental Notation Systems**
Definition: Universal Numbering System (US, primarily): #1–32 for permanent teeth (upper right molar = #1, ending lower right molar = #32); primary teeth A–T. FDI (International): two-digit system: first digit = quadrant (1–4 permanent, 5–8 primary), second = tooth position (1–8 from midline). Palmer notation: grid with numbers 1–8 per quadrant with directional brackets.
Key relationships: Quadrants (UR, UL, LL, LR), primary dentition (20 teeth: 4 incisors + 2 canines + 4 molars per arch), permanent dentition (32 teeth including third molars/wisdom teeth), mixed dentition (transition period age ~6–12 years).

**C03 — Oral Histology: Enamel**
Definition: Enamel: hardest biological tissue (Vickers hardness ~340 VHN), 96% mineral (hydroxyapatite: Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆(OH)₂), 1% organic, 3% water. Produced by ameloblasts (lost at eruption — enamel cannot regenerate). Organized into enamel prisms (rods, ~4–5 μm diameter) running from DEJ to surface. Hunter-Schreger bands: alternating prism orientations increase fracture resistance. Gnarled enamel at cusps for occlusal stress resistance.
Key relationships: Enamel solubility in acid (caries mechanism), demineralization-remineralization equilibrium, fluorapatite (fluoride substitution → acid resistance), enamel hypoplasia (developmental disruption), enameloplasty, prism direction (affects cavity preparation design).

**C04 — Dental Caries (Decay)**
Definition: A biofilm-mediated, diet-modulated, multifactorial infectious disease resulting in demineralization of tooth hard tissue by organic acids produced by bacterial fermentation of dietary carbohydrates. Streptococcus mutans (primary cariogenic bacteria) and Lactobacillus spp. are key. Caries risk factors: fermentable carbohydrate frequency, saliva flow and composition, fluoride exposure, oral hygiene, socioeconomic status.
Key relationships: Stephan curve (pH drop after sugar exposure → critical pH 5.5 for enamel dissolution, 6.2–6.7 for dentin/cementum), biofilm (plaque), remineralization (saliva + fluoride), ICDAS (International Caries Detection and Assessment System, scores 0–6 by lesion severity), caries lesion (initial → cavitated).

**C05 — Dental Plaque Biofilm**
Definition: Structured community of microorganisms embedded in an extracellular polysaccharide matrix adherent to tooth surfaces. Supragingival plaque (above gingival margin): primary caries; primarily Streptococcus, Actinomyces. Subgingival plaque (below gingival margin): periodontal disease; anaerobic Gram-negative bacteria (Porphyromonas gingivalis, Treponema denticola, Tannerella forsythia — "Red complex").
Key relationships: Pellicle (protein layer on tooth, basis for plaque adhesion), glucan matrix (produced by S. mutans from sucrose via glucosyltransferase), biofilm maturation (initial colonizers → co-aggregation → late colonizers), calculus (mineralized plaque — mechanical retention).

**C06 — Periodontal Anatomy**
Definition: The periodontium (supporting structures): gingiva (keratinized gingival epithelium + connective tissue), periodontal ligament (PDL, dense fibrous connective tissue connecting cementum to alveolar bone, ~0.2 mm wide, contains Sharpey's fibers, proprioception, and progenitor cells), cementum, alveolar bone.
Key relationships: Gingival sulcus (normal depth 1–3 mm, lined by sulcular epithelium — permeable), junctional epithelium (attaches to enamel at base of sulcus), alveolar crest, furcation (junction of roots in multi-rooted teeth), biologic width (minimum 2 mm of healthy attachment above alveolar bone — critical for restoration margins).

**C07 — Periodontal Disease**
Definition: Spectrum of inflammatory conditions of the periodontium caused by the host immune response to subgingival plaque biofilm. Gingivitis: reversible inflammation of gingiva without bone loss (redness, swelling, bleeding on probing, no attachment loss). Periodontitis: irreversible destruction of periodontium including attachment loss + alveolar bone loss; classified by stage (I–IV: severity) and grade (A–C: rate of progression and risk factors).
Key relationships: Probing depth (PD) vs. clinical attachment level (CAL), bone loss (radiographic), furcation involvement (Hamp class I–III), tooth mobility, bleeding on probing (BOP), systemic connections (DM, cardiovascular disease, adverse pregnancy outcomes — bidirectional relationship), AAP/EFP 2017 classification.

**C08 — Dental Pulp Pathology**
Definition: Pulp conditions on spectrum from healthy to necrotic. Reversible pulpitis: inflammation reversible with removal of stimulus; mild sensitivity (cold especially, relieved when stimulus removed). Irreversible pulpitis: intense, spontaneous, lingering pain (especially to cold and heat); requires root canal or extraction. Pulp necrosis: death of pulp (may be asymptomatic or cause periapical abscess/swelling). Cracked tooth syndrome: incomplete fracture causing stimulus-related pain.
Key relationships: Pulp testing (cold test, EPT — electric pulp tester), pain quality (sharp, radiating, lingering — indicates irreversible), vitality vs. periapical status, periapical pathology (PDL widening, periapical radiolucency on X-ray).

**C09 — Endodontic Treatment (Root Canal Therapy)**
Definition: Removal of diseased or necrotic pulp tissue from root canals, followed by shaping, disinfection, and three-dimensional obturation (sealing) of the canal system. Goals: remove microbial reservoir, seal canal to prevent reinfection, preserve tooth. Steps: access cavity → canal length determination (EAL ± radiograph) → canal shaping (rotary Ni-Ti files) → irrigation (NaOCl, EDTA, CHX) → obturation (gutta-percha + sealer).
Key relationships: Working length, apical terminus (within 0.5–1 mm of radiographic apex), rotary Ni-Ti files (ProTaper Gold, WaveOne — reciprocating), irrigation (NaOCl: 1–6%, antimicrobial + tissue dissolution; EDTA: chelation for smear layer removal), obturation (cold lateral condensation vs. warm vertical compaction), post-endodontic restoration (full coverage crown typically required for posterior teeth).

**C10 — Dental Radiography**
Definition: Key radiographic types: Periapical (PA): shows whole tooth + periapical area; gold standard for endodontic and periapical assessment. Bitewing: shows coronal portion of upper and lower teeth simultaneously; primary tool for interproximal caries detection and alveolar bone height assessment. Panoramic (OPG): full arch view — less detail, screening, surgical planning. CBCT (Cone Beam CT): 3D imaging; gold standard for implant planning, jaw pathology, complex endodontics.
Key relationships: Radiation dose (PA ~0.001–0.008 mSv; panoramic ~0.01–0.025 mSv; CBCT 0.05–0.5 mSv depending on FOV); ALARA; digital X-ray (30–50% dose reduction vs. film); selection criteria (ADA/AAOMR guidelines — based on clinical findings, not routine).

**C11 — Restorative Materials: Dental Composites**
Definition: Tooth-colored resin composites: bis-GMA or TEGDMA methacrylate resin matrix + inorganic filler (silica, barium glass, zirconia — 60–80% by weight). Light-cured (photoinitiator: camphorquinone, activated 460–480 nm blue light). Properties: good esthetics, technique-sensitive adhesive protocol required, polymerization shrinkage (0.5–3.5% — stress at bonded interface), radiopacity (diagnostically important).
Key relationships: Filler particle size (nanofill: <0.01 μm, high polish; hybrid: 0.4–1 μm, general purpose; microhybrid; bulk-fill for 4–5 mm depth), polymerization shrinkage stress, moisture sensitivity, wear resistance, bonding system required (etch-and-rinse vs. self-etch), shade selection.

**C12 — Dental Ceramics**
Definition: Ceramic materials used for esthetic restorations. Feldspathic porcelain (conventional PFM crowns — porcelain fused to metal for strength). Leucite-reinforced ceramic (IPS Empress, pressed — conservative, esthetic). Lithium disilicate (IPS e.max: 400–600 MPa flexural strength, excellent esthetics — crowns, veneers). Zirconia (Y-TZP: 900–1200 MPa — posterior crowns, implant-supported restorations; high-translucency zirconia for esthetics).
Key relationships: CAD/CAM manufacturing (Cerec, 3Shape Dental System — digital impressions → milling), bonding (HF etching + silane for ceramics, MDP-based primers for zirconia — chemical bonding), fracture toughness vs. esthetics trade-off, ceramic-tooth adhesion, cement selection (resin cement vs. glass ionomer vs. zinc phosphate).

**C13 — Dental Implants**
Definition: Titanium (Grade IV CP Ti or Ti-6Al-4V alloy) or zirconia screw-shaped devices surgically placed into alveolar bone to serve as artificial tooth roots. Osseointegration (direct structural and functional connection between living bone and implant surface, Brånemark's discovery, 1965 Gothenburg) enables load transfer. Implant types: endosseous (in bone — standard), subperiosteal (obsolete).
Key relationships: Osseointegration, surface treatment (sand-blasted acid-etched — SLA, oxidized — TiUnite, hydrophilic — SLActive — improves early osseointegration), implant-abutment connection (internal hex, morse taper — reduces micromovement/microgap), healing time (3–6 months traditionally; immediate loading protocol in favorable cases), ISQ (implant stability quotient: resonance frequency analysis).

**C14 — Prosthodontics**
Definition: Restoration and replacement of missing teeth and oral structures. Fixed prosthodontics: crowns (single tooth coverage), bridges (FPD — fixed partial denture, uses adjacent teeth as abutments), implant-supported crowns/bridges. Removable prosthodontics: complete dentures (edentulous patients — acrylic resin base + denture teeth), partial dentures (RPD: metal framework + acrylic saddles for partially dentate patients).
Key relationships: Preparation design (taper 2–6°, minimum occlusal clearance 1.5–2 mm for metal, 2 mm for ceramic; axial wall height for retention), impression materials (polyvinyl siloxane PVS, polyether), temporary restorations, cementation (glass ionomer, zinc phosphate, resin cement — selection based on situation), pontic design (ridge lap, modified ridge lap, ovate).

**C15 — Orthodontics**
Definition: Prevention and correction of malocclusion (improper tooth and jaw relationships). Angle's classification: Class I (normal molar relationship), Class II (mandible retrognathic — overjet increased), Class III (mandible prognathic — negative overjet/underbite). Treatment: removable appliances (Hawley retainers, functional appliances), fixed appliances (brackets + archwires — metallic or ceramic), clear aligners (Invisalign — series of clear polyurethane trays).
Key relationships: Tooth movement types (tipping, bodily translation, rotation, intrusion, extrusion, torque), force delivery (0.5–5 N for tooth movement), anchorage, retention (post-treatment — lifelong retainer typically), growth modification (Class II — Herbst, Twin Block; Class III — reverse pull headgear in growing patients), orthognathic surgery for skeletal discrepancies beyond dental compensation.

**C16 — Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery (OMFS)**
Definition: Surgical specialty managing conditions of the mouth, jaws, face, and neck. Procedures: simple extractions (forceps, elevators), surgical extractions (bone removal, sectioning), third molar/wisdom tooth removal, dental implant placement, bone grafting (autograft, allograft, xenograft, alloplastic), orthognathic surgery (Le Fort I osteotomy — upper jaw; BSSO — bilateral sagittal split osteotomy — lower jaw), temporomandibular joint (TMJ) surgery, tumor excision.
Key relationships: Socket healing (blood clot → granulation tissue → woven bone → lamellar bone, 6 months for full healing), dry socket (alveolar osteitis, 2–5% of extractions, 20% of lower third molars), inferior alveolar nerve (risk in lower third molar surgery), Le Fort levels (I through III: levels of maxillary fracture classification), PRP/PRF (platelet-rich plasma/fibrin for enhanced healing).

**C17 — Oral Pathology and Oral Cancer**
Definition: Oral potentially malignant disorders (OPMDs): leukoplakia (white patch, cannot be scraped off, 1–5% malignant transformation), erythroplakia (red patch, >25% carcinoma in situ or SCC — higher risk), oral lichen planus (autoimmune, erosive form has ~1% malignant transformation). Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC): 90%+ of oral cancer; major risk factors: tobacco (smoking + smokeless), alcohol (synergistic), HPV-16 (oropharyngeal cancer especially). Survival: 5-year OS ~65% overall; <35% if detected at advanced stage.
Key relationships: Most common sites: lateral tongue + floor of mouth (most dangerous site), buccal mucosa, gingiva; early detection (visual exam + toluidine blue/VELscope adjuncts); biopsy (incisional — gold standard diagnosis); TNM staging; multidisciplinary treatment (surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy).

**C18 — Fluoride in Dentistry**
Definition: Fluoride prevents caries by: (1) remineralization of early carious lesions (ionic fluoride → fluorapatite, more acid-resistant); (2) inhibition of cariogenic bacterial enzymes (enolase inhibition reduces acid production); (3) topical effect predominates over systemic. Sources: fluoridated water (0.7 mg/L optimal in US), fluoride toothpaste (1,000–5,000 ppm NaF/SnF₂/MFP), professional fluoride varnish (22,600 ppm NaF), gel/foam (9,000–12,000 ppm), silver diamine fluoride (SDF, 38% — arrests active caries, stains lesion black).
Key relationships: Enamel fluorosis (cosmetic effect from excess systemic fluoride during enamel formation, age 0–6 — TF scale 0–9), optimal fluoride range, water fluoridation effectiveness (~25% caries reduction), fluoride toxicity (acute: nausea, vomiting — 5 mg NaF/kg; chronic: skeletal fluorosis), SDF indications (high-risk patients, non-restorable caries).

**C19 — Local Anesthesia in Dentistry**
Definition: Most common dental anesthetic: lidocaine 2% with 1:100,000 epinephrine (vasoconstrictor extends duration, reduces systemic absorption). Articaine 4% (crosses dentin better — buccal infiltration for mandibular molars). Prilocaine 3% (without vasoconstrictor for patients where epinephrine contraindicated). Key injections: inferior alveolar nerve block (IANB) + lingual nerve for lower teeth, infiltration for upper teeth and lower anteriors.
Key relationships: pKa (lidocaine 7.9 — fast onset in infected tissue where pH is lower), maximum dose (lidocaine 4.4 mg/kg; articaine 7 mg/kg), vasoconstrictor considerations (cardiac patients — limit epi, not eliminate), allergic reactions (rare with amides — most dental LAs are amides; esters now rarely used), failed anesthesia (irreversible pulpitis → inflamed pulp has lower threshold).

**C20 — Dental Public Health**
Definition: The dental specialty focused on preventing oral disease and promoting oral health in communities. Key issues: oral health disparities (children in poverty 5× more likely to have untreated caries), access to care (dental uninsured ~74 million in US), water fluoridation (cost-effective community prevention), school-based sealant programs, integration of oral health into primary care, oral health literacy.
Key relationships: DMFT/DMFS index (Decayed-Missing-Filled Teeth/Surfaces — standard epidemiological measure), WHO Global Oral Health Programme, Healthy People 2030 (US), dental safety net (FQHC, dental schools), telehealth/teledentistry, oral health equity.

**C21 — Temporomandibular Disorders (TMD)**
Definition: Conditions affecting the TMJ, masticatory muscles, and associated structures. Classification (DC/TMD): myofascial pain, arthralgia, disc displacement (with reduction: reciprocal click; without reduction: limited opening), degenerative joint disease (OA), subluxation. Epidemiology: 10–15% population affected; predominantly women in reproductive years.
Key relationships: TMJ anatomy (condyle + articular disc + glenoid fossa + articular eminence), disc displacement with reduction (click on opening and closing — disc recaptures; TMJ arthrogram/MRI), occlusal splint (stabilization, Michigan-type: first-line TMD treatment), behavioral modification, physiotherapy, NSAIDS, in severe cases: arthrocentesis, arthroscopy.

**C22 — Pediatric Dentistry**
Definition: Dental specialty for infants, children, adolescents, and patients with special healthcare needs. Unique considerations: early childhood caries (ECC: caries in children <6 years, bottle caries pattern — maxillary anterior teeth; associated with nursing and bottle habits), space maintenance (maintaining arch length after premature primary tooth loss), behavior management (tell-show-do, nitrous oxide sedation, protective stabilization, general anesthesia for uncooperative children or extensive treatment).
Key relationships: Primary dentition eruption sequence (mandibular central incisors first, ~6 months; all primary teeth by ~3 years), permanent eruption (first molars + incisors ~6 years — "six-year molar"), pulp therapy in primary teeth (indirect pulp cap, pulpotomy — formocresol or MTA, not full RCT), stainless steel crowns (gold standard for primary molar restoration), fluoride supplementation (where water not fluoridated).

**C23 — Dental Materials: Adhesive Dentistry**
Definition: Modern restorative dentistry relies on adhesive bonding of restorations to tooth structure. Enamel bonding: acid etching (37% phosphoric acid, 15–30 sec) creates microretentive surface; bonding agent infiltrates; resin tags form. Dentin bonding: more complex — hybrid layer (collagen network infiltrated by adhesive); total-etch (3-step or 2-step) vs. self-etch (1-step or 2-step) systems.
Key relationships: Etch-and-rinse bond strength to enamel (>30 MPa); dentin bond strength (20–40 MPa); hybrid layer (Nakabayashi 1982); collagen degradation (MMP — matrix metalloproteinases — degrade hybrid layer over time: bond durability challenge); water tree (nanoleakage), chlorhexidine (MMP inhibitor → preserves hybrid layer).

**C24 — Oral Systemic Connections**
Definition: Oral health is bidirectionally connected to systemic health. Periodontitis — Diabetes: bidirectional (DM increases periodontitis risk and severity; periodontitis increases insulin resistance and glycemic control difficulty). Periodontitis — Cardiovascular disease: oral bacteria (P. gingivalis) found in atheromatous plaques; shared inflammatory pathway. Oral bacteria — Aspiration pneumonia: especially in elderly/institutionalized. Pregnancy: periodontal disease associated with preterm birth and low birth weight (controversial, conflicting evidence).
Key relationships: Systemic dissemination of oral bacteria (bacteremia during procedures), endocarditis prevention (prophylaxis for high-risk cardiac conditions — AHA guidelines), oral manifestations of systemic disease (DM: xerostomia, candidiasis; HIV: oral lesions; Sjögren's: xerostomia), gingival manifestations of hematologic disease.

**C25 — Dental Implant Complications and Peri-Implant Disease**
Definition: Peri-implant diseases: Peri-implant mucositis (reversible inflammation of tissues surrounding implant — analogous to gingivitis: BOP, edema, no bone loss). Peri-implantitis (irreversible — loss of supporting bone around implant + BOP + suppuration — analogous to periodontitis). Prevalence: ~15–35% of implants/43–47% of patients after 10 years.
Key relationships: Implant surface contamination (role of titanium surface in inflammation), biofilm management, peri-implant bone levels (radiographic monitoring), treatment (decontamination — air abrasive, laser, mechanical; surgical access; bone grafting for defect fill), implant failure (early: osseointegration failure; late: peri-implantitis or occlusal overload), survival vs. success criteria.

## 5. Patterns

**P01 — Caries Risk Assessment and Management**
Description: Categorize patient risk (CAMBRA — Caries Management by Risk Assessment): assess risk factors (high sugar frequency, active caries, low saliva flow, poor hygiene, no fluoride, low socioeconomic status, special needs) and protective factors (fluoride use, good saliva, good hygiene, sealants); classify low/moderate/high/extreme risk; prescribe risk-appropriate protocol (recall interval, fluoride level, dietary counseling, remineralization agents: MI Paste/casein phosphopeptide-ACP, xylitol).
When to use: New patient examination; routine recall assessment; pediatric patients.
Example: High-caries-risk child: 2+ new lesions in past year, frequent juice consumption, no fluoride toothpaste, low-socioeconomic family; prescribe: 1.1% NaF toothpaste (Rx), fluoride varnish every 3 months, SDF on active lesions, nutritional counseling, 3-month recall.

**P02 — Periapical Diagnosis Protocol**
Description: Systematic pulp/periapical diagnosis: history (pain quality, duration, spontaneous vs. provoked); clinical exam (soft tissue, sinus tract, tenderness to palpation, tenderness to percussion); vitality testing (cold, EPT); radiographic assessment (PDL widening, periapical lucency, root resorption); correlate findings → diagnosis (normal, reversible pulpitis, symptomatic/asymptomatic irreversible pulpitis, necrotic pulp, normal periapex, symptomatic/asymptomatic apical periodontitis, abscess).
When to use: Every tooth presenting with symptoms; before any endodontic procedure.
Example: Tooth #30: spontaneous severe pain, worse with heat, not relieved when heat removed, periapical radiolucency; EPT no response; diagnosis: pulp necrosis + symptomatic apical periodontitis; treatment: non-surgical RCT.

**P03 — Implant Treatment Planning**
Description: Clinical evaluation (medical history, bone volume estimate, adjacent teeth, occlusion, smile line, patient expectations); radiographic planning (CBCT for bone volume/density, vital structure proximity — IAN, sinus); prosthetic planning (backward planning: start from ideal prosthetic position, determine ideal implant position); evaluate need for bone augmentation; informed consent; staged or simultaneous approach; select implant diameter/length to maximize primary stability.
When to use: All implant cases; surgical guide fabrication.
Example: Missing #19: CBCT shows 8mm bone height above IAN; select 8mm implant length (1mm safety margin); width adequate for regular diameter (4.1mm); bone grafting of buccal dehiscence anticipated; plan 4-month healing before final crown; digital guided surgery with surgical stent.

**P04 — Periodontal Treatment Protocol**
Description: Phase 1 (Cause-Related Therapy): oral hygiene instruction + motivation; full-mouth debridement (scaling and root planing — SRP, quadrant by quadrant); systemic antibiotics if indicated (metronidazole + amoxicillin for generalized stage III/IV grade C); re-evaluation at 6–8 weeks; assess BOP%, pocket depths, CAL. Phase 2 (Surgical): for residual pockets >5mm not resolving with phase 1 → osseous surgery, regeneration (GTR, bone graft), mucogingival surgery. Phase 3 (Maintenance): supportive periodontal therapy every 3–4 months for periodontitis patients.
When to use: Any patient diagnosed with periodontitis.
Example: Generalized stage III grade B periodontitis: 45% BOP, mean PD 5.2mm, radiographic bone loss to mid-root on multiple teeth; phase 1 full-mouth SRP over 4 visits; re-evaluation: BOP 18%, mean PD 3.8mm, 8 residual pockets >5mm → surgical referral for those pockets; 3-month SPT.

**P05 — Extraction Complication Prevention**
Description: Review medical history for bleeding disorders, anticoagulant therapy (manage per AAOMR/ADA/ACCP guidelines — typically do not discontinue warfarin for simple extraction, maintain INR <3.5; DOAC timing management), bisphosphonate therapy (MRONJ risk — no evidence-based consensus for cessation); perform atraumatic extraction technique; place socket graft if ridge preservation needed; post-operative instructions (gauze pressure, avoid smoking/suction, soft diet, chlorhexidine rinse).
When to use: All extraction planning and execution.
Example: Patient on warfarin for AF (INR 2.8): simple extraction planned; local hemostatic measures (oxidized cellulose, sutures, tranexamic acid mouthwash); verify INR < 3.5 day of procedure (no dose change); post-op instructions on bleeding management.

**P06 — Complete Denture Fabrication Sequence**
Description: Primary impressions (stock trays + alginate) → primary casts → custom trays → definitive impressions (selective pressure technique, zinc oxide eugenol or polyvinyl siloxane) → master casts → record bases + occlusal rims → jaw relationship records (centric relation, vertical dimension of occlusion, face bow transfer) → denture tooth setup → wax trial denture try-in (esthetics, phonetics, function check) → processing → delivery → review appointments.
When to use: Complete denture fabrication for edentulous patients.
Example: 72-year-old edentulous patient: 8-appointment sequence over 8 weeks; centric relation recorded with reinforced ZOE paste; 33° posterior teeth selected for masticatory efficiency; try-in: patient approves esthetics; processed; delivery: occlusal adjustment; 24-hour, 1-week, 1-month reviews.

**P07 — Orthodontic Bracket Placement Protocol**
Description: Tooth cleaning and pumice prophylaxis; apply etchant to bonding area (15–30 sec); rinse, dry lightly; apply primer (moisture-tolerant); apply adhesive to bracket base or tooth; position bracket per anatomical bracket placement guidelines (height, angulation, torque); light-cure 10–20 seconds per bracket; remove excess adhesive; verify with mirror and explorer.
When to use: Fixed orthodontic appliance placement.
Example: Direct bonding with self-ligating brackets (Damon): pumice all teeth; 37% phosphoric acid etch 15 sec; rinse thoroughly; apply Ortho Solo primer, no cure; place Transbond XT adhesive on bracket pad; position at bracket height (FA point of tooth); cure 20 sec; complete arch → archwire placement (0.014 NiTi for leveling/alignment phase).

## 6. Anti-Patterns

**AP01 — Prescribing Antibiotics for Dental Pain Without Infection**
Why wrong: Antibiotics do not relieve dental pain of pulpal origin and do not treat reversible or irreversible pulpitis, which requires mechanical removal of the inflamed/necrotic pulp (root canal) or extraction. Antibiotic prescribing without clear evidence of spreading infection (cellulitis, lymphadenopathy, trismus, fever, systemic signs) contributes to antimicrobial resistance. Dentists prescribe ~10% of all US outpatient antibiotics, with high rates of inappropriate prescribing.
What to do instead: Diagnose and treat the cause (root canal, extraction, incision and drainage of abscess); prescribe antibiotics only for spreading infections with systemic signs; first choice: amoxicillin 500 mg TID × 5–7 days for odontogenic infection in non-penicillin-allergic patients.

**AP02 — Ignoring Systemic Medical History in Treatment Planning**
Why wrong: Many systemic conditions directly affect dental treatment: bisphosphonate therapy (MRONJ after invasive procedures), anticoagulants (bleeding risk), uncontrolled DM (impaired healing, infection susceptibility), immunosuppression (infection risk, healing), cardiac conditions (infective endocarditis risk — prophylaxis indicated for specific high-risk conditions per AHA), hypertension (epinephrine caution), bleeding disorders.
What to do instead: Conduct thorough medical history review at every appointment; update at each recall; consult with patient's physician for complex medical conditions; modify treatment plan and technique based on medical findings; document decision-making.

**AP03 — Placing Restorations Without Removing All Caries**
Why wrong: Placing a restoration over remaining carious dentin seals bacteria into the tooth, allowing continuing caries progression beneath the restoration (secondary caries). The restoration may appear intact while caries progresses silently to pulp involvement. Applies to both "watching" a lesion that has become cavitated and incomplete caries removal before restoration.
What to do instead: Remove all soft, infected dentin before final restoration in teeth with vital pulps; in teeth with large deep cavities approaching the pulp, consider indirect pulp cap (minimal excavation, biocompatible liner — MTA or calcium hydroxide, monitoring) rather than accidental pulp exposure; use caries detection dyes to identify remaining infected dentin.

**AP04 — Taking Dental X-Rays on a Fixed Schedule Regardless of Risk**
Why wrong: Historically, dental offices applied fixed recall X-ray intervals (bitewings every 12–24 months) to all patients. Low-risk adult patients with no caries, good oral hygiene, and no risk factors do not require radiographic examination at fixed intervals regardless of clinical findings. The ALARA principle requires justification for each exposure.
What to do instead: Apply ADA/AAOMR patient selection criteria: base radiographic prescription on clinical findings (new patient, risk assessment, clinical signs/symptoms) rather than a calendar; low-risk adults may require bitewings every 18–36 months; high-risk patients with active caries may need 6–12 month intervals.

**AP05 — Overloading Implants Through Poor Prosthetic Planning**
Why wrong: Dental implants require careful biomechanical planning. Cantilever extensions, excessive occlusal table width, parafunctional habits (bruxism), and misaligned implant-prosthetic axis create unfavorable off-axis forces leading to screw loosening, implant fracture, abutment fracture, and marginal bone loss.
What to do instead: Apply backward planning from prosthetic goals to determine implant position; minimize cantilever; design prosthesis within implant envelope; assess and manage parafunction (nightguard); use larger diameter implants for posterior loading; verify axial loading at delivery.

**AP06 — Using Amalgam Removal as Treatment for Non-Specific Symptoms**
Why wrong: Claims that amalgam fillings cause various non-specific systemic symptoms (fatigue, memory problems, multiple chemical sensitivity) and should be removed to relieve these symptoms are not supported by evidence. Amalgam removal releases more mercury than leaving the restorations in place; there is no evidence-based indication for amalgam removal in asymptomatic patients based on presumed mercury toxicity.
What to do instead: When replacement is indicated for restorative reasons (fracture, secondary caries, esthetic), use appropriate mercury-safe removal protocol; for patients with concerns, explain the scientific evidence; refer to appropriate medical specialist if symptoms warrant investigation; do not perform amalgam removal purely for non-dental symptom relief.

**AP07 — Restoring Teeth With Poor Periodontal Prognosis Without Addressing Periodontitis First**
Why wrong: Placing crowns, bridges, or implants adjacent to teeth with active periodontal disease, or as abutments on teeth with compromised bone support, leads to restorative failure (crown margin exposure as bone loss continues, abutment tooth loss, peri-implantitis from periodontal bacteria). "Building on sand."
What to do instead: Treat and stabilize periodontal disease first; re-evaluate prognosis after phase 1 therapy before finalizing restorative plan; extract teeth with hopeless prognosis before prosthodontic work; ensure peri-implant tissue health before loading; integrate periodontal maintenance into overall restorative and prosthetic plan.
